2026 Election Watch — How the Vote Is Run and Counted

Snapshot: 2026-10-24; dirty-tricks update 2026-10-31 (rows 15–18 and AK row 1); Sunday update 2026-11-01 (rows 19–26, CA, AK, monitors, Stevens); election-eve refresh 2026-11-02 (AK suit dismissed Nov 1; SCOTUS denied DOJ’s CA request Nov 2; Stevens endorsed Rogers). Election: Tue Nov 3.

Here’s why this matters

  • States and counties run elections, not Washington. County clerks count, county boards certify, and secretaries of state certify statewide. The federal government’s formal role is narrow: enforcing voting-rights law and sending observers.
  • This year the federal government is unusually active. DOJ is sending about 1,000 monitors, suing states over voter rolls and ballots, and sued to block certification of the Alaska Senate race if the Republican loses. A federal judge dismissed that suit Nov 1 (DOJ is appealing), and the Supreme Court on Nov 2 denied DOJ’s emergency bid to stop California counting about 20,000 ballots. So far the courts have turned DOJ down on both.
  • Certification is a required duty, not a choice. Every county refusal since 2020 ended in certification, either by court order or because officials backed down. A refusal still causes delay, lawsuits and “stolen election” stories.
  • Slow counts are normal. In states that count mail ballots late, early leads can flip (the “red mirage” or “blue shift”). A change in the lead is not evidence of fraud.
  • 2028 runs through the people elected now. The officials and county boards chosen this year, and the precedents set this year, will administer the next presidential election.

DOJ’s rules for monitors: observe only, no firearms, no talking to voters inside polling places, follow state law. Several states (CO, PA, TX, SD) say monitors have no more access than the public unless a court order or state approval gives them more.

Certification & counting

  • The law: In every state, certification is ministerial, meaning officials must certify and have no discretion to refuse. Courts can order it (mandamus). California (2025) and Michigan have written this into law explicitly; in Michigan the state board takes over if a county misses its 14-day deadline.
  • Georgia State Election Board: The 2024 rules for hand-counting ballot totals and for “reasonable inquiry” before certification were struck down, and the GA Supreme Court upheld the block in June 2025. No new 2026 SEB rule was found in current reporting. Counties certify by 5 p.m. the Monday after the election; the state certifies within 17 days.
  • Arizona: Cochise County supervisors were prosecuted over the 2022 delay (Judd pleaded guilty). Maricopa’s divided administration (recorder vs. board) is the operational risk.
  • New federal pressure points: DOJ suits aimed at certification (AK) and at counting ballots (CA) are new. Before 2026, certification fights were local.

Election officials & deniers

  • For secretary of state races see 2026 State Races — Governors, Election Officials, Legislatures, Courts. Election deniers on the ballot include Jim Marchant (NV SoS), Alexander Kolodin (AZ SoS) and Andy Biggs (AZ governor).
  • Turnover and threats: In Brennan Center’s 2024 survey, 38% of local election officials reported threats or harassment and 1 in 5 said they were unlikely to serve through 2026. The Bipartisan Policy Center put turnover in 2024 at 41%, the highest in 25+ years. No 2026-specific survey was found.
  • Inside the administration: Heather Honey is DHS deputy assistant secretary for election integrity. In February she told election officials there would be “no ICE presence at polling locations.”
