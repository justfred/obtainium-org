2026 Ballot Measures — National Watch List (outside CA)

Snapshot: 2026-10-24. Election: Tue Nov 3. California: see 2026 California Propositions — Watch List.

Here’s why this matters

Ballot measures let voters set policy directly on abortion, voter ID, district maps and taxes. They can also change who turns out for close races on the same ballot.

  • Missouri is the epicenter. Amendment 3 would repeal the abortion rights voters approved in 2024, and Prop A decides whether the GOP map splitting Kansas City takes effect.
  • The redistricting war is on the ballot in Missouri, Maryland and Colorado. The results shape future House seats.
  • Direct democracy itself is on the ballot. North and South Dakota would raise the bar for amendments to 60%; Missouri and Montana would protect the initiative process. These get little attention, but they decide what future ballots can do.
  • The turnout lever is real but modest. Research finds each initiative adds roughly 1–2 points of midterm turnout, mostly when the measure is contested and well-funded (see the evidence section below).

Bottom line

  • Missouri is the epicenter. Amendment 3 (repeal 2024 abortion rights + ban trans care for minors) is a coin flip (43–44, SLU/YouGov Aug), and Prop A decides whether the GOP’s 2025 gerrymander that splits Kansas City takes effect (polls split three ways). Amendment 6 would lock in the result.
  • Abortion is quieter than in 2022/2024: 4 measures. Three would protect abortion rights (VA Q1, NV Q6 second vote, ID Prop 1 in a deep-red state, polling 61%), and one would roll them back (MO Amd 3).
  • Election-rules measures dominate the GOP side: photo-ID amendments in NV, OH, NC, AZ and CO (mail-ID number), plus “citizens-only voting” in 6 states. Those are mostly symbolic and poll well (NV Q7 65–24). They are the clearest test of Fred’s turnout-lever theory. On the progressive side the election measures are VA Q3 (felon voting rights) and MA Q4 (same-day registration).
  • The redistricting war is on the ballot: MO Prop A, MD Q3 (a Democratic gerrymander enabler, losing 25–53 in UMBC’s Oct poll) and CO Amd 86 (GOP-backed block on a Democratic mid-decade redraw, needs 55%).
  • Direct-democracy restrictions (ND M1 and SD Amd L, which raise the bar for amendments to 60%; UT’s 60% bar for tax initiatives; ID HJR 4) versus protections (MO Amd 6, MT CI-133). These are low-salience, and how they turn out will shape future cycles.

By theme

  • Abortion: 4 measures. Three would protect abortion rights (VA Q1, NV Q6, ID Prop 1) and one would roll back the 2024 win (MO Amd 3, a toss-up). Turnout energy is muted compared with 2022–24 (Sabato).
  • Voter ID / citizenship: Photo-ID constitutional measures in NV, OH, NC, AZ (Prop 144) and CO (Amd 84, mail-ballot ID number). Citizens-only voting in AK, AZ, AR, KS, SD, WV. Most restate existing law. They poll 60%+ and are symbolic turnout plays.
  • Redistricting: MO Prop A (the most consequential), MD Q3 (a pro-Dem gerrymander that looks likely to fail), CO Amd 86 (an anti-Dem-redraw block that needs 55%). VA’s mid-decade amendment was decided at an April 21 special election and is not on this ballot.
  • Ranked-choice / primaries: AK M2 (repeal, a toss-up after the 2024 near-miss), MA Q3 (adopt top-two), plus local RCV measures in Honolulu and Longmont, CO.
  • Minimum wage / paid leave: None found statewide in 2026. VT Prop 3 (constitutional right to collective bargaining) and MD Q1 (fund union contracts) are the labor items.
  • Marijuana: MA Q8 would roll back legal sales (55% oppose). ID HJR 4 would bar initiatives on drug legalization. No legalization measures found.
  • Taxes: Progressive taxes are on defense (WA I-645 repeal) and offense (CO Amd 87). Anti-tax measures: NC 3.5% cap, IA 2/3 vote to raise income taxes, FL homestead (needs 60%), TN property-tax ban, UT 60% for tax initiatives, CO 136.
  • Guns: AR Issue 2 (gun rights as a “fundamental” right), MA Q9 (veto referendum on the 2024 gun law, ~70% keep). The ID state-gun vote is novelty.
  • Immigration: CO Amd 81 (ICE notification, needs 55%). AZ Prop 317 (cartels as terrorists; critics warn of border militarization).
  • Trans issues: MO Amd 3 (bundled), CO 134/135, AZ 318, NE sports, WA I-638 and parental-notification initiatives. VT Prop 4 adds gender identity to equal protection (pro-LGBTQ).
  • Direct democracy: Restrictions in ND M1, SD Amd L, UT (tax initiatives), ID HJR 4, CO Amd 85 (8th-grade reading level). Protections in MO Amd 6 and MT CI-133. Context: Kansans rejected Supreme Court elections 61–39 on Aug 4.

Turnout-lever theory — evidence so far

  • Some support for a general effect: Tolbert & Smith found each initiative raised midterm turnout by roughly 1.2–1.7 points. The effect is strongest when measures are salient and well-funded.
  • The canonical case is weaker than its reputation: Smith et al. (2006) found the 2004 same-sex-marriage bans did not measurably drive turnout once competitive races were controlled for. Other analyses find effects in swing states and midterms. CIRCLE warns that mobilization campaigns, not the measure itself, do the work.
  • Split tickets undercut “reverse coattails”: In 2024, abortion measures won in states that elected anti-abortion candidates. Missouri 2024 is the cleanest example: Amendment 3 passed (~52%) while Trump and Hawley won easily (from memory, unverified). In 2022, measure votes outran Democratic candidates, and more ballots were cast at the top of the ticket than on the measure.
  • Ohio Aug 2023 Issue 1 (GOP attempt to raise the amendment threshold to 60% before the abortion vote) backfired. It failed ~57–43 with unusually high August turnout, and that mobilization carried into the Nov 2023 abortion win (from memory, unverified). Measures meant to change the rules can energize the other side.
  • Implication for 2026: The GOP-side ID and citizenship measures are near-certain passes with no opposition campaign, so expect little new turnout from them. Contested, funded fights (MO Amd 3, AK M2, MO Prop A) are where campaign activity, not the measure itself, could move margins in close races.

Caveats

  • Polling is thin. Many measures (VA, NV Q6, AK M2, CO 86/87, MT) had no 2026 public poll found. Sponsor-funded polls are noted as such: MI Prop 2 (PPP), OH Issue 3 (Honest Elections Project Action), MD Hart (pro) and Protect Maryland Votes (con).
  • Measure numbers for several states (AR, KS, WV, NC, NE, WA, UT) are taken from secondary guides. Confirm on Ballotpedia or the state SoS.
  • Litigation is pending for MO Prop A (SCOTUS appeal), CO Amd 87 (lawsuit) and the NE sports measure (state supreme court).
  • No statewide measures were found for TX, ME, MN, SC, PA or NH in November 2026. NH’s school-tax-cap votes are local. Searches were limited, so absence isn’t confirmed.
  • The “realistic outlook” column is a judgment from the polls cited, not a forecast.
