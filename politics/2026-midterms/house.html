2026 House — Competitive Races

Snapshot: 2026-11-02 (election eve; first written 2026-10-24). Election: Tue Nov 3. 🏠 = Fred’s district (CA-48)

Archived 2026-11-07. This page is an election-eve snapshot and will not be updated further. The closing update below gives the results as of Nov 7; the full results are in the sources named there.

Closing update (2026-11-07): Democrats have the House, 254 to 162 with 19 races uncalled

  • Count: Democrats 254, Republicans 162, 19 uncalled (AP via Yahoo, Nov 7). CBS estimates a final range of 251 to 268 Democratic seats. NBC’s estimate is D 259, R 176 on a different (projected) basis, so the two are not comparable.
  • Flips: Democrats flipped at least 24 Republican-held seats; Republicans flipped none (CNBC, Nov 4). Democrats lead the national House vote by nearly 13 points (Cook, via Yahoo).
  • 🏠 CA-48 (Fred’s district): called for Marni von Wilpert (D), 106,972 votes (59.3%) to Jim Desmond (R) 73,502 (40.7%), per California Secretary of State semi-official results as of Nov 6 and an AP projection. An open seat after Issa retired.
  • Still uncalled (partial list; I could not find all 19): CO-4, Eileen Laubacher (D) leads Lauren Boebert (R) 48.8% to 47.9% (3,172 votes) with 97% counted; Colorado counties have until Nov 13. CNBC named ME-2 as a possible Republican flip.
  • Versus the forecasts: Decision Desk HQ projected 241 Democratic seats and Silver Bulletin gave Democrats about 93 to 95% to win the House. The result beat the projection. See 2026 Midterms — Realistic Forecasts & Nate Silver.

Sources: search summaries and page fetches by Claude on 2026-11-07 (AP counts via a Yahoo live blog and NBC/CBS/CNBC trackers; no AP page opened). Numbers move daily, so check AP for the latest.

Here’s why this matters

The House writes the budget and tax bills, and the majority controls investigations, subpoenas and impeachment.

  • The majority is thin. Republicans hold 218 seats, exactly a majority. Democrats need a net gain of about 4 from today’s seats.
  • The maps changed mid-decade. Texas, Florida, Ohio, North Carolina, Louisiana, Tennessee and Alabama redrew for Republicans; California (Prop 50) and Utah went the other way. On paper that nets Republicans roughly 6–9 seats, but in a strong Democratic year several of the new GOP seats are competitive (see the backfire watch below).
  • The forecasts agree here more than on the Senate. Every major model favors Democrats, from DDHQ 79% (Oct 26) to Silver ~93–95%; prediction markets sit at ~94–95% on election eve.
  • Fred’s district is on the list. CA-48 is an open seat that Prop 50 made bluer.

Bottom line

  • Now: 218 R – 214 D – 1 I (Kiley, CA-6, left GOP Mar 2026) – 2 vacant (FL-20 D, TX-23 R) as of Oct 24. Not reconciled; vacancies may have been filled or counted differently.
  • Majority: 218. Dems need net +3 to +4 from today’s seats (sources differ, see below) — but redistricting adds roughly 6–9 more on paper.
  • Changed since Oct 30 (as of Nov 2): markets firmed up (Kalshi 93.8¢, Polymarket 95¢ for D; Kalshi was ~91% a week earlier); Cook made another round of moves (5 more ratings on Mon Nov 2 per Yahoo/Cook) and now projects D flips of 12–25 seats, up to 240; Silver’s generic ballot D+9. No new DDHQ or Silver House % found (see below).

Election night update (2026-11-03, ~7 PM PT) — PROVISIONAL

Source: search snippets of live trackers (CNN, CNBC, CBS, MS NOW, Florida Politics, NBC). No page opened; partial counts; verify against AP or a live tracker before relying on any figure.

  • Direction: CNBC headline “blue wave hits House”; CNBC projects Democrats at least 205 seats, MS NOW floor D 210 / R 160. CNN: top Republican sources fear early returns point to a Democratic sweep of both chambers. This matches the forecasts (D ~230–240), not a surprise.
  • Called for Democrats in Trump-won seats: KY-6, Zach Dembo (Trump +15 in 2024, per CNN); CO-8, Manny Rutinel over Rep. Gabe Evans (R). KY-6 is the kind of seat that signals a wave.
  • Flipped: NC-11, Jamie Ager (open GOP seat, not an incumbent defeat).
  • Held: Deborah Ross, Valerie Foushee (NC); Jared Moskowitz (FL-25), AP call 8:07 PM ET, about 51.2–51.4% to Scott Singer’s 46.3–46.7% with ~97% in. He moved from FL-23 into the redrawn, more competitive FL-25 and won a third term anyway, a point for the “gerrymander backfire” watch below.
  • Still close: FL-13 Luna (R) and FL-27 Salazar (R) both led by under 1 point right after polls closed. No result found.
  • No confirmed House Republican incumbent upset found yet (one search hit was a 2024 story).
  • Candidate to watch, added by Fred: Melat Kiros (D, CO-1, Denver). A democratic socialist (Justice Democrats-backed) who beat 15-term Rep. Diana DeGette in the June 30 primary by 20,000+ votes. Not a competitive seat in November: early count 78% Kiros, 14% Christy Peterson (R), 6% Shimon Blau (I), and the call came the same night. KKTV reports she’d be the first Black woman to represent Colorado in Congress. Sworn in Jan 3, 2027. Sources: CPR, KKTV, Colorado Newsline. Search snippets only; not opened.
  • Not checked: CA races (polls close 8 PM PT), CA-48 (Fred’s district), the 9 redrawn states except FL, and the TX new seats.
