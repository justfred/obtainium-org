Here’s why this matters

Voters, not the legislature, have the final word on these, and what voters pass is hard to undo.

  • Prop 40, the billionaire tax, is the biggest money fight in the state and a national test of wealth taxes. Props 41 and 42 are poison pills: if either passes with more Yes votes than 40, it kills 40 even if 40 passes. Prop 42’s ban on wealth taxes is permanent.
  • Prop 39 (voter ID) is the main reason Republicans turn out. Even a loss creates a national talking point.
  • Prop 45 (CEQA) is the biggest housing and infrastructure policy change on the ballot.
  • The governor’s race isn’t close, so these measures are what’s left to drive turnout. That matters for close House seats like CA-22 and Fred’s CA-48.

Bottom line

  • The real fight is 40 vs 41/42. Prop 40 (billionaire tax) is under 50% in almost every September and October poll, and its lead has shrunk from about 7 points to roughly even. Opponents have spent about $304M (through Oct 20) against roughly $32M in favor. Realistically it leans No. Even if it passes, 42 could outpoll it and kill it.
  • Prop 45 (CEQA) is the sleeper. It went from 73% Yes in PPIC’s July poll to 49–46 in September. Erosion that fast usually ends in a No. It’s a toss-up that leans No.
  • Prop 39 (voter ID) leans No, but pollsters disagree. IGS and PPIC have it down 12–13 points; NBC/Telemundo and SurveyUSA had it up. It’s the measure most likely to bring out Republican voters.
  • Probably decided already: 3, 37, 2 (Yes); 5, 44 (No). Props 1 and 38 (bonds) lean Yes but are soft. Prop 4 and Prop 43 have about 30% undecided, and confused voters usually vote No.
  • Turnout: The governor’s race isn’t close (Emerson, Oct 17–19: Becerra +27). That means the props, mainly 39 and 40, are what’s left to drive turnout. Prop 40 is 68% Yes among voters under 30, so it only passes if young voters turn out.

Competing measures: 40 vs 41 vs 42

  • Rule (Cal. Const. Art. II §10(b)): If conflicting measures both pass, the one with more Yes votes prevails.
  • Prop 40 only survives if it passes AND gets more Yes votes than any of 41/42 that also pass. For example, 40 at 51% and 42 at 53% means 40 dies.
  • The math is bad for 40. Both counter-measures poll at or above 40 in PPIC’s survey. In IGS, 42’s support (43%) is near 40’s (45%), but 42’s undecideds lean conservative. Newsom backing 42 gives cover to Democrats who are against the billionaire tax.
  • Even a clean win isn’t final. Walters (CalMatters, Oct 22) lists what comes after: the poison-pill vote count, then state and federal lawsuits, then Franchise Tax Board valuation problems, then residency disputes over billionaires who left after Jan 1.
  • For Fred’s vote: A No on 41 and 42 counts as much as a Yes on 40.

Caveats

  • There’s no October PPIC or IGS poll yet. Most data is from September, before most of the ~$1M/day ad spending against 40.
  • Pollsters disagree a lot on 39 and 40. Manhattan Institute (R) and FM3 (D) are partisan sponsors, and IVP’s survey asked its question after giving respondents the LAO estimate.
  • PPIC’s undecided share is unusually low (it pushes respondents to choose), which inflates both Yes and No compared with IGS.
  • The money figures mix different cutoff dates (Oct 5 FPPC vs. Oct 20 Capital & Main). Opposition money for 37, 38, 41, and 42 wasn’t reported in the sources found.
  • Prediction-market odds (Defirate) come from a jumbled page. I’m treating them as directional only.
  • The Politico Oct 19 voter ID poll is cited, but I couldn’t retrieve its numbers.
