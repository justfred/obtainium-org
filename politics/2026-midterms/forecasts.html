2026 Midterms — Realistic Forecasts & Nate Silver

Snapshot: 2026-10-24. Companion to 2026 Senate — Competitive Races / 2026 House — Competitive Races. Goal: weight realistic models over single polls.

Bottom line

  • Senate: realistic range 49–54 D, most likely 51–52. Dems favored ~60–75% — but a 2-pt correlated polling miss toward R flips it back.
  • House: realistic range ~224–240 D. Dems ~90%+ even with a 2016-size miss.
  • Polls in midterms have been accurate (2018, 2022 error < 1 pt). The big pro-D misses were presidential years (2016, 2020, 2024). The 2025 off-year missed toward R (NJ by ~9).
  • Fundamentals agree with polls: generic ballot D+8–9; Dems running +10.4 over 2024 margins in state-leg specials (MultiState, Oct 14).

Nate Silver says

  • Odds (Oct 22): House 95% D, Senate 76.4% D, avg 52–48. Senate low point was 50.1% on Sep 7 — it has climbed since.
  • Race odds (~Oct 6): NC Cooper 95% · TX Talarico 74% · OH Brown 74% · ME Jackson 71% · AK Peltola 65% · KS toss-up (51% R).
  • Why he’s above the raters: expert ratings “tend to implicitly assume a neutral political environment” until late; his model corrects for that.
  • Fake polls (Oct 21–22): dropped 15–17 fabricated Wedgewood polls that leaned ~1 pt D. MI moved D+3.6 → D+3.0. “Overall, our forecast is little changed.”
  • On polling-error direction (Oct 23 podcast): rejects The Economist’s claim that error favors D in blue states / R in red states — “The sun coming up is… a more consistent pattern than systematic polling error.”
  • On the GOP: “I don’t think I’ve ever seen worse vibes from a party.” Nebraska: Ricketts’s own internal shows him only +3 — bad sign in a state far redder than Texas.
  • What it would take for R (Aug): economic rebound, “too far left” backlash, or a “systemic 2016-style polling error.” A big miss → “Forget about the Senate,” but Dems likely still win the House.
  • Model mechanics: errors correlated nationally, by state, and by demographics; partisan/internal polls assumed ~4 pts biased; “slightly fat-tailed.”
  • Redistricting (early 2026): “something of a wash so far”; heavy gerrymanders could become “dummymanders” in a big D year. (Oct commentary paywalled.)

Polling error — what to expect

  • A typical miss is ~2 pts either way, and correlated — close races move together.
  • With ~8 Senate races within 4 pts, a uniform 2–3 pt R-ward miss turns 52–53 D into ~48–49 D. Harry Enten (CNN, ~Oct 3): a 2–4 pt R miss means Dems hold only NC and GA of the competitive set.
  • Nate Cohn (NYT, Oct 19): “absolutely possible” polls overestimate Dems, but “no reason to assume” it — they didn’t in 2018 or 2022, and underestimated Dems “by a lot” in 2025.
  • R side: Patrick Ruffini finds Senate polls overstated D by ~2.6 pts across four cycles (unverified, via partisan outlet).
