Fact check: “The Two Pennies” on Harry Truman (“An honest public servant can’t become rich in politics”)

What is this about?

A claim-by-claim check of a long Facebook post from the page The Two Pennies (Oct 23, 2026) that tells Harry Truman’s post-presidency as a story of integrity, built on two quotes.

Written by Claude, an AI assistant, and published by Fred Heald. It is Claude’s analysis, not Fred’s views. Checked 2026-10-30 from search snippets only (no pages fetched). The post’s text is not reproduced here.

One-sentence overview

The biography is mostly standard and the financial hedging in the middle is fair; the opening scene (Truman “drove” home with no motorcade) is contradicted by the Truman Library; and of the two quotes the story rests on, the diary line is documented (April 1954, secondhand) while the “unless he’s a crook” line has no traceable source.

Claim table

Claim Verdict Notes
On Jan 20, 1953 Truman got in a car with Bess and “drove home to Independence”; “No motorcade. No staff car.” False as stated Truman Library photo records and the National Park Service say he attended Eisenhower’s inauguration, then left Union Station by train, with Bess and Margaret, with a crowd holding thank-you signs; he greeted a crowd on arrival in Independence on Jan 21. (A later road trip in 1953 in which he drove himself is from memory; not found in these results.)
No security detail because former presidents had no right to one in 1953 Plausible; not checked Lifelong Secret Service protection for former presidents came decades later (from memory).
Born May 8 1884 in Lamar, Missouri; mule trader/farmer’s son; WWI artillery captain; haberdashery with Eddie Jacobson failed by 1922; paid down debt Standard biography; not checked here Widely documented.
Diary, April 24 1954: “An honest public servant can’t become rich in politics” Supported (secondhand); day not confirmed TruthOrFiction cites Off the Record: The Private Papers of Harry S. Truman, p. 306, an April 1954 diary entry, with this wording. I did not read the Library’s transcript or the book, and no source gave the 24th. (The book’s editor was Robert Ferrell, the same historian who attacked Plain Speaking; from memory.)
Plain Speaking (Merle Miller): “No man can get rich in politics unless he’s a crook” Unverified; likely misattributed TruthOrFiction found no evidence Truman said it; Wikiquote lists it as disputed; Wikipedia editors could not trace it to a speech, letter or book; the closest documented line is the diary sentence above, which has no “crook” in it. That page did not address Plain Speaking; one other result says the line is cited to Miller’s 1973 book, which I could not confirm. Context on the book: historian Robert Ferrell (“Plain Faking,” American Heritage, 1995) argued Miller fabricated many quotes in Plain Speaking; David McCullough questioned its accuracy; Miller said the interviews were real and many on tape. Some readers say it’s partly reliable. The post cites the book without noting any of this. Net: of the two quotes carrying the weight, the diary line holds up and the “crook” line does not.
Truman declined corporate boards and offers to commercialize the presidency (“they want the presidency”) Plausible; not checked here A well-known Truman Library theme.
Military pension of $112.56 a month, the only federal income Partly $112.56 appears in one source, $111.96 in another.
Memoirs: “substantial advance,” net “modest” Supported; hedge is fair Advance reported at $600,000 to $670,000 (sources differ); taxes of 35 to 50 percent and assistants left a small net (Mental Floss; Fayette County Record). A Los Angeles Times column (via Star Tribune) argues Truman overstated his distress. The post’s own line, “not as precarious as the narrative implied,” is accurate.
1958 Former Presidents Act gave $25,000 a year, office and staff; Truman “the direct cause” Partly $25,000 is right. CRS says the act was prompted largely by Truman’s financial difficulties, but Hoover was also living and covered, and one source disputes the origin of the $25,000 figure (Carnegie offer, “reportedly”). “Direct cause” is stronger than the sources.
LBJ signed Medicare in Independence and gave Harry and Bess the first two cards (1965) Standard; not checked here Well documented (from memory).
Same house, morning walks, “just anybody again” Not checked
Died Dec 26 1972, age 88; Plain Speaking published the next year Standard; not checked here The 1973 timing is part of Ferrell’s criticism.
“An honest public servant can’t become rich in politics” as a universal rule Value claim; not checkable The post says “can’t,” not “can’t easily.”
Implied: present-day officials should be measured against this (the “tag someone who needs to know” call) Inference Not stated; the contrast is left to the reader.

Pattern notes

  • Nostalgic virtue tale with an implied present-day contrast. No named target; the closing call (“Tag someone who needs to know”) invites readers to supply one.
  • Self-hedging in the middle, overridden at the end. The post admits the “financial reality was more complicated than the legend,” then closes: “He meant the word can’t.”
  • Two quotes carry the weight; one is documented, one is not. The diary sentence is real; “unless he’s a crook” is the version that circulates widely and cannot be traced.
  • A concrete scene opens it (“got in a car... drove”), the kind of detail that makes it feel true; this one is wrong.
  • Style (tentative, not a verdict): repeated “Truman” where a person would write “he” is common in machine-generated text; I did not test it.
  • Left out (from memory, not checked): Truman’s rise through Kansas City’s Pendergast machine (personally honest, politically connected).

Open

  • Truman Library transcript or Off the Record p. 306 to confirm the exact day and wording (secondhand only).
  • Whether Plain Speaking actually contains the “crook” line (a text search of the book).
  • Whether the 1953 summer road trip is what the “drove” detail comes from.
