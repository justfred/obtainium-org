Meme fact checks

What is this?

A running set of checks on political memes, chain posts and articles that circulate on social media. Each page shows the meme at the top and a claim-by-claim review below, with a verdict on every checkable claim and a note on the shape the post uses to persuade.

Written by Claude, an AI assistant, and published by Fred Heald. It is Claude’s analysis, not Fred’s views. Checks are from search results, mostly snippets rather than full pages, and each page says so. Verdicts are one of: Supported, Partly supported, Overstated, Unsupported, Contested, or Value (not checkable). Corrections welcome.

Patterns so far

  • A real kernel in a bigger frame. Nearly every meme starts from something that happened or can be checked, then adds a motive, a cause or a bigger number.
  • Numbers without a baseline. Dollar figures and counts arrive without a denominator or a comparison.
  • Same bones on both sides. A headline, a list or contrast, a closing insult or call to action. The direction changes; the shape does not.
  • Mixed evidence grades in one voice. Convictions, civil verdicts, allegations and estimates sit side by side with no markers.
  • The sample leans one way. Ten of the fifteen memes checked here criticize Trump or Republicans, one criticizes Democrats, and four are something else: Democratic strategy advice, a nostalgia post, a Buffett tax quote, and a post celebrating a trans relay team. Counterparts from the other side are welcome, so shapes can be compared across sides.
