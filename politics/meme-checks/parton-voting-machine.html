Fact check: “Dolly Parton’s sister says a Tennessee voting machine kept switching her vote to Blackburn”

Verdict at a glance: Core story supported; “rig,” “15 times turnout” and “no officials answered” not.

What is this about?

A claim-by-claim check of a Facebook meme (page “Really American,” Oct 21, 2026) saying a Tennessee voting machine kept switching Dolly Parton’s sister’s vote to Blackburn.

Written by Claude, an AI assistant, and published by Fred Heald. It is Claude’s analysis, not Fred’s views. Checked 2026-10-30 from search snippets only (no page fetched; no official finding found).

One-sentence overview

The core story is real (voters reported the screen selecting Blackburn), but “rig” and “15 times turnout” go beyond the evidence, “officials haven’t answered” is stale, and officials’ explanation (touchscreen calibration or unintended touch) fits what was reported.

Claim table

Claim in the meme Verdict Notes
Stella Parton said a machine at the Hermitage Library kept defaulting to Blackburn; she corrected it 3 times Supported (that she said it) Reported by Mediaite, Newsweek, Daily Beast, IBTimes; she posted on Facebook. Whether the machine malfunctioned: not established.
“I would never vote for her for anything”; “Don’t let them rig this election” Supported as her quotes “Rig” is her word, not a finding. No investigation result found.
Two other voters in Williamson County described the same thing Partly Jackie Herrud (Longview Rec Center, Spring Hill, Williamson) is reported by WSMV. A second reported voter was at the Bellevue Library (Davidson County), not Williamson. Poll workers “heard similar complaints”: her statement, not verified.
Nashville turnout is 15 times the 2022 midterms Unsupported Nearest figure found: Aug 2026 primary, first five days of early voting in Davidson County, 14,760 vs 3,646 in 2022 (about 4x; Pamphleteer, citing state files). No November early-voting total found. 15x not found anywhere.
“Tennessee officials have not yet answered requests for comment” Stale or false Tennessee Coordinator of Elections Mark Goins: “not a vote flipping situation,” normally an unintended touch. Williamson County administrator Chad Gray: no discrepancies in the equipment. Davidson’s John Richardson: can count on one hand the calls out of ~65,000 early voters. Timing vs the Oct 21 post not established.
The machine “picked the Republican instead” (implied: on its own) Contested; officials’ explanation is plausible Davidson, Williamson and Madison counties use ES&S ExpressVote touchscreens. Officials and an election expert cite calibration issues on older touchscreens; Davidson put out coffee stirrers for voters who need them. No evidence of deliberate or widespread flipping (WLRN, Tennessee Lookout). Not found: whether Parton’s machine was tested or recalibrated.
Implied: this is rigging (headline “BREAKING,” “Don’t let them rig this election”) Unsupported Motive claim. A calibration offset sends touches to a neighboring button; Blackburn and Green are adjacent candidates in the same race (inference, not checked against the ballot layout). Check both directions: are there reports of flips toward Green? Not found either way.
“Election officials in other states have brushed off vote-flipping claims in past cycles” True but loaded Similar claims recur since at least 2008 and 2020 and have usually traced to calibration or user error; the meme’s wording implies a cover-up.

What holds up and what the meme leaves out

  • Holds up: several voters said the screen showed the wrong choice, and Parton’s advice to check every screen is sound. The voters caught it because they reviewed it; Tennessee’s system shows a review before the ballot is cast (generic ExpressVote design, from memory, not checked for this county).
  • Left out: the officials’ responses, the calibration explanation, the scale (a handful of reports against about 65,000 early voters in Davidson), and the 15x number’s source.
