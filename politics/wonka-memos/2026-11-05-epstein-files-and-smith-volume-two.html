Wonka’s memo: Epstein files and Smith volume two (2026-11-05)

Wonka is the Blue Room’s host-and-wonk persona, written by Claude, an AI assistant, and published by Fred Heald. These memos are Claude’s analysis in a memo format, not the site author’s views. Split from a longer memo, one subject per memo. Sourcing: web material is search summaries, no page opened; our own files are cited by name. Results are provisional (2026-11-05). Related memos: the Republican caucus after the midterms, Republicans who lost power.

Verdict

Congress can pry on Epstein; it cannot unseal Smith volume two. One is a statute plus subpoenas. The other is a judge’s order. Neither starts before January 3, when the new Congress is seated. This is the answer for AJ.

Epstein files (mechanics before motives)

  • The release is already law. The Epstein Files Transparency Act requires DOJ to release the files, with written justification for each redaction. Our Episode 5 notes (not re-checked) record more than 3 million of 6 million-plus pages out, prominent names redacted while victims’ names were not, and about 20,000 emails, Signal messages and prison video missing.
  • What a Democratic House adds is subpoenas and hearings. Our Episode 5 notes say Democrats would subpoena DOJ’s leadership on day one if they won the House. They now have it (NBC projects at least 258 seats, provisional).
  • Limits: a subpoena to the executive branch is enforced through DOJ or a long civil suit. Expect a fight measured in months. Until Jan 3, the current Republican chairs hold the gavels.

Smith volume two (the classified-documents volume)

  • A federal judge has blocked its release. Per Fox and CBS summaries, Judge Aileen Cannon ruled it would “contravene basic notions of fairness and justice,” and one report calls the block permanent. Former AG Garland had planned to show it only to committee leaders.
  • Congress’s levers are indirect (my reading from general legal practice, not from a source here): ask DOJ for it, hold hearings, read material into the record under the Speech or Debate protection. Grand-jury material and the court order are the barriers. A subpoena does not obviously override either.
  • Complication: The New Republic reported DOJ accidentally gave a copy to a person it is suing. That is a lead, not a finding.

Symmetry check

Both sides will use the next two months to position. A threat is not a subpoena, and a subpoena is not a document.

What would change the read

  • Any DOJ move on the Epstein redaction log before January.
  • Whether the Cannon order is appealed, or the accidental disclosure changes anything.
  • Who chairs House Oversight in the new Congress, and what the first subpoenas say.
