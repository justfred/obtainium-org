Wonka’s memo: Will the “vote the bums out” coalition hold at the next election? (2026-11-05)

Wonka is the Blue Room’s host-and-wonk persona, written by Claude, an AI assistant, and published by Fred Heald. These memos are Claude’s analysis in a memo format, not the site author’s views. Interpretation (unconfirmed): the anti-incumbent, anti-Trump coalition that voted in 2026, holding in the 2028 presidential election. Sourcing: search summaries only, no pages read in full; final exit-poll data was not found.

Related memos: what Trump can do after the midterms, Republicans most likely to break with Trump.

Verdict

No, not as stated. “Vote the bums out” is a coalition against whoever holds power, so it has no standing orders for 2028. Its two glue ingredients, disapproval of Trump and a sour economy, belong to the incumbent. In 2028 Trump is not on the ballot, and the “bums” will be whoever Democrats elected. Call it a lease, not a deed.

What the coalition was made of (preliminary)

  • Disapproval of Trump. Early CBS exit polls put his approval near 38%, on track for the lowest of any president in a midterm (CBS).
  • Economic pain. Voters were more than twice as likely to say their finances were worse than better (same early exit polls).
  • Independents. Pre-election polling had only 23% of independents approving of Trump (YouGov, September). I found no exit data showing how independents actually voted.

Why it may not carry over

  • Swing voters were not warm to Democrats. One poll had swing voters backing Democrats by 12 points while still viewing Republicans less unfavorably than Democrats. Another (Cygnal, a Republican-leaning pollster) had them leaning Republican, 38 to 35. The polls conflict, so treat the picture as unsettled.
  • The 2025 precedent cuts both ways. Two-thirds of New Jersey voters said they were dissatisfied or angry, and Democrats won on the economy and cost of living (ABC). The same mood punishes the party in power, and from January that includes Democrats in the House.
  • Midterms are not presidential electorates. Turnout shifts, and the 2026 coalition is a midterm electorate.

My guess (judgment)

The durable parts are the age and education realignments. The perishable parts are Trump-specific anger and the economy.

What would change the read

  • Final exit polls and precinct-level data on independents.
  • Whether Democrats show a governing record by mid-2027.
  • The economy through 2027.
  • The 2028 nominees, on both sides.

Sources

  • CBS News exit polls, NPR/Marist, Sept 2026, ABC on 2025 voters, Newsweek on swing voters, Cygnal Aug 2026
