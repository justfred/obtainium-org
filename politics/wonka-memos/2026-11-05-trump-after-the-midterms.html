Wonka’s memo: what Trump can do after the midterms (2026-11-05)

Wonka is the Blue Room’s host-and-wonk persona, written by Claude, an AI assistant, and published by Fred Heald. These memos are Claude’s analysis in a memo format, not the site author’s views. Sourcing: search summaries plus one page read in full where noted; results are provisional (2026-11-05). Unverified items are marked.

Related memos: Trump’s triumphal arch and its legal challenge, Republicans most likely to break with Trump.

Verdict

Trump keeps the powers Congress can’t reach: the veto, executive orders, pardons, firings, DOJ priorities and foreign policy. He loses Republican control of the budget, subpoenas and confirmations on January 3. Until then he has a lame-duck window.

Where he is now

Conciliatory so far. He told the New York Times he would “absolutely” accept the results. He suggested he’ll “get along, probably” with Democrats while reminding reporters he still has the veto. He acknowledged the Iran war and gas prices hurt Republicans, and Vance blamed the war too (CBC, CNN). He also blamed Republican candidates for the losses (PBS headline).

The bounds

Democrats can’t touch Democrats can touch from Jan 3
The veto (overriding needs two-thirds) The budget (spending bills pass both chambers)
Executive orders, pardons, firings Subpoenas and hearings
DOJ priorities and contempt enforcement Impeachment (House); conviction needs 67 senators
Foreign policy and the Iran war (war powers resolutions get vetoed) Confirmations, with a Democratic Senate (reported 53 to 54, provisional)
Tariffs under his other authorities Tariff-emergency resolutions (the Senate passed several in Oct 2025; the House blocked votes)

The courts remain the main outside check.

What I expect (judgment), using his 2018 pattern

In 2018 he fired the attorney general the day after the midterms, then fought a shutdown and declared an emergency over the wall.

  1. A lame-duck rush through Jan 3: confirmations, orders, rules and pardons. Republicans are planning one last reconciliation bill and clashing over how far to push his agenda (The Hill); Newsweek reports Thune floated a Supreme Court confirmation in the lame duck.
  2. Vetoes and a funding standoff, with shutdown threats as leverage.
  3. Stonewalling oversight: executive-privilege claims, and DOJ declining to enforce contempt.
  4. Symbolic projects like the arch, which are cheap, executive-controlled and fast.
  5. Blaming candidates rather than the war or the economy.

What might be at risk

The Memorial Circle site and its sight line, comity with a Democratic Congress, any Republican whose seat is next, agencies he targets for cuts, and the norm that a president accepts a congressional check.

What would change the read

  • A new executive order wave, or a firing, in the next week.
  • The shape of the lame-duck package, and a Supreme Court vacancy.
  • Whether he honors subpoenas after January 3.
