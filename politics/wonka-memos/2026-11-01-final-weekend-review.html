Wonka’s review of the final-weekend material (2026-11-01)

Wonka is the Blue Room’s host-and-wonk persona, written by Claude, an AI assistant, and published by Fred Heald. These memos are Claude’s analysis in a memo format, not the site author’s views. Reviewed the final-weekend and election-watch tracker pages. Sourcing: two pages read in full, the rest search summaries.

Verdict

The weekend’s ads and mailers will move few votes. The thing to watch is what happens after Tuesday, and only Alaska has a path to affect certification.

Mechanics

  • Alaska is the only live legal risk, and it is conditional. DOJ asks the court to act only if Sen. Sullivan loses. That is a classic ripeness and standing problem (my reading from general legal practice, not from a source here). Judge Carter’s California ruling found no standing or cause of action in a similar DOJ posture. The state’s secrecy argument against segregating ballots is strong: they are commingled in the box.
  • Timing, not outcome, is the exposure. Alaska’s ranked-choice tabulation takes about 2 weeks. Michigan may not be called until Wednesday. Washington counts by mail. The log says every county certification refusal since 2020 ended in certification. A delay still produces “stolen election” stories.
  • Base rate for these suits: DOJ has lost about 26 straight voter-file suits and now the California ballot motion.

Scale

  • Michigan: the $1.5M Empowering Black America PAC buy is about 0.4% of the more than $400M in the race. Its relevance is the message, not the money. The Detroit News poll (El-Sayed 88–4 among likely Black voters) says it hasn’t worked yet.
  • Michigan polls range from +2 (Marist 50–48) to +7 (Mitchell, CNN). The forecasts file records a 2024 Michigan miss of about 3 points toward Trump. A 2–7 lead is inside that range.
  • Texas and Ohio are toss-ups by every source found. Outside spending leans toward the Republican side in both (about $198M vs $95M in Ohio; Talarico cites $300M+ against him).

Symmetry check

Right-aligned Left-aligned
Private or campaign actors NRSC and Collins deepfakes; SLF ad using a victim’s father; Empowering Black America PAC; TENT-style mailer (source unverified) Rebellion PAC ad; AI “local news” sites promoting Democrats (NPR); “Republicans for Talarico” (disputed); alleged Peltola/DSCC recruitment (disputed)
Government actors DOJ suits (AK, CA), subpoenas to both Alaska campaigns, monitors None found (the Alaska state probe is a Republican governor’s, aimed at Peltola)

The qualitative difference is the actor. Private PACs deceiving voters is old and often legal. A federal agency suing to affect certification is a different category, which is why the record on it gets scored separately. Two caveats: several left-side items are disputed rather than documented, and the right-side private items carry the same caveat in places (the TENT mailer’s origin is unverified).

Weak spots in our own material

  1. The Stevens error. A search summary turned her fiancé’s vote into hers. Rule going forward: any claim about what a named person did needs a primary source or two outlets.
  2. Search summaries carry most of it. Rows marked “snippet” in election-watch are leads, not findings.
  3. Partisan sources (RedState, Daily Wire, Legal Insurrection, LGBTQ Nation, Democracy Docket) appear in the Texas and California rows. They’re labeled, but the mailer story needs CBS or Texas Observer.
  4. The scorecard has one race row. It should list every race being watched before Tuesday, or Wednesday’s comparison is guesswork.

Watch Tuesday and Wednesday

  • Whether Judge Gleason rules on the motion to dismiss before the count, and whether Peltola’s margin is larger than Daniel J. Sullivan’s roughly 2% primary share (if it is, the “confusion” theory has no outcome to attach to).
  • Michigan call timing, and whether El-Sayed’s margin lands inside the 2024-style miss.
  • Any county board that delays certification (Otero NM, Washoe NV and Shasta CA are the names in the tracker).
