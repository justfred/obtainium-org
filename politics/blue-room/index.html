Wonka Good evening. I'm Bill Wonka, and I'm told I'm to tell you about a show.

It has a host (me), four guests who disagree about almost everything, and a bell. I use the bell once.

Every episode we take one issue and sort it into three piles: documented, disputed, and somebody said it with great conviction. The guest chair knows how things actually work. I know which day it is, most of the time.

Please take your seats. The seats are real. The experts are not.

It's not a debate. It's a fact-check with a studio audience.

How the Blue Room Works

Standing Rules of the Panel, adopted unanimously by a host who was not asked.

Article I. The Host. The host shall be gentle, ironic and wrong at least once per episode, on the record, in front of witnesses. The host shall keep the bell and shall use it with restraint, or with what passes for restraint. Maximum occupancy is one host, four guests and one guest chair. The management is not responsible for personal belongings left in the studio, including strings.

Article II. The Panel. Four regular guests shall argue one issue in good faith, or what the production considers good faith. No guest shall speak for more than one-fifth of the evening. The host is exempt, by tradition and by volume.

Article III. The Guest Chair. An expert shall be seated each week, introduced last, with ceremony. The experts are invented. Their lines rest only on the sources listed in the notes, and where the sources run out the expert shall say so, in a voice of great seriousness.

Article IV. The Ledger. At the close of each episode, every claim made on stage shall be sorted into one of three categories: documented, disputed or unsupported. A claim does not become documented by being said loudly. This has been tested.

Article V. The Notes. Sources and links shall be provided so the public may check our work, and is encouraged to do so. Hand sanitizer is provided in the form of footnotes. Where a figure was not re-confirmed, the notes shall say so, with feeling.

Article VI. Notice. The Blue Room is a work of fiction, written by Claude, an AI, as political satire. The host and guests are invented comic archetypes, not real people, and nothing a character says is the opinion of the site author. The applause is provided by the writers. No real person was harmed in the making of this program, though one string was frayed.

Done this first day of November, two thousand twenty-six. By order of the Host. ★
