The Blue Room — Episode 11: The Callback

Wonka Good evening, and welcome to the Blue Room. Tonight we do the thing television almost never does, which is go back and check. Eleven days ago this panel made predictions, and we promised to score them. Everyone is here, including our guest chair from that night, and one of you has packed a box. [laughter]

  • Walt Dunning, the retired actuary, has brought the original ledger, a ruler, and a fresh red pen. [laughter]
  • Dale Truitt has a corkboard with eleven days of string on it and has asked that we not look at the middle. [laughter]
  • Margo Bell has been retroactively right about several things and would like to explain how. [laughter]
  • Shelby Spinner, the red-hat regular, has a cardboard box on her lap and a note on her palm. It says “BYE.” She underlined it. [a few groans, a few cheers]
  • And back in the guest chair, Hector Almeida, the former state elections director, who predicted that the most dangerous words in an election are “it’ll be quick,” and has been proven correct by two Senate races. [applause]

Wonka Five slides. Most of it is search summaries, and I’ll say where it isn’t.

1. The Senate. AP projected Democrats won control late on Nov 3. The seat count is unsettled across sources: one fetched report says at least 54, another says at least 51-49. [1]

2. The House. AP called it for Democrats early Wednesday; at least 224 seats expected, some races still counting. [2]

3. Two still open. Alaska: Peltola led Sullivan by 637 first-choice votes of about 216,000; neither is at 50 percent; the next state count is Nov 18. Mississippi: Colom was ahead by 65 votes on Nov 4; no automatic recount law. [3]

4. Michigan. The AP called the Senate race for El-Sayed at 2:27 a.m. Wednesday. [4]

5. Certification. No county refusal found so far. Georgia’s county deadline is reported as Nov 9 by one guide and Dec 7 by another. [5]

Wonka The Michigan race was called on Thursday, and—

Hector Wednesday. About half past two in the morning. [laughter]

Wonka I said I’d be wrong once. I have my one. [laughter]

Walt I’d like to open the ledger. Row five, Margo’s. She said Michigan wouldn’t be called until Wednesday. It was called Wednesday at 2:27 a.m. That’s technically right. I’d mark it “right, barely,” because the prediction was a long wait and what we got was a long night.

Margo Right is right. [laughter] Step one: I said Wednesday. Step two: it was Wednesday. Step three: I’d like to thank Michigan for not making me explain “technically.” And the chyron part of my prediction is unscorable, because nobody can measure a violation of the Geneva Conventions with a ruler. [laughter]

Dale Row six is mine. I said three people would say the count was rigged before the West Coast closed, and two of them would be winning. I looked for it, and I found nothing from election night. A senator did say something about “rigging” days later, about one specific alleged scheme in Alaska. That’s a different dot. I’d call it not found, and I’d like it noted that I wanted to be right more than anyone here. [laughter]

Hector Row eight is mine, and it’s not yet scorable. I said every state certifies on time and a few counties hold a dramatic meeting. I found no refusals so far, but I also found no certification reports. The deadlines run into December. Dull is the point, and dull isn’t done yet. [scattered applause]

Walt Row four, the box of ballots in the closet. I looked for a post-election box story from November 3 to 5 and found nothing. There were older cases and an unrelated dumpster story in October. I’d put it as “no sighting,” which isn’t the same as a miss.

Wonka Yes, and “I couldn’t find it” is a different column from “it didn’t happen.” We’re careful about that here. [laughter]

Margo Rows nine and ten, my subpoena and Dale’s shutdown, are future. The House is Democratic, the new Congress convenes January third, and I found no subpoena news yet. The stopgap I found runs to December eleventh, so if a shutdown comes, it might come earlier than Dale said.

Dale Earlier than spring. That’s, I’d like it noted, an upgrade. [laughter]

Wonka Which brings us to row seven. Shelby, the floor is yours, and it’s a complete turn. I’m told the bell is retiring, and I’d like to ring it once, for ceremony. [the bell rings; applause]

Shelby Thank you, Wonka. [laughter] Row seven says Republicans keep the Senate. I said that, and I said it with a pen I wouldn’t cap. The models said Democrats were favored, about sixty to seventy-five percent. I said last year’s off-year elections missed toward Republicans, which was true, and I said it would flip the answer, which wasn’t. I lost the Senate, I lost the bluff, I picked the casino floor, and I asked for a recount. I’d like it on the record that I’d rather lose to the facts than to the pigeon. [laughter, applause]

SHELBY (cont.): As a mom, one last time. I’m taking some time off to be with my kids. They’ve been very patient. And this panel taught me something I didn’t plan to learn: you can believe a thing very hard and still write down the number. Walt, thank you for the citations. Margo, I’ll miss being annoyed at you. Dale, call me if the string goes anywhere. Hector, make it boring. And Wonka: the binder goes in the lost-and-found, and I expect a receipt. [laughter, long applause]

Wonka You get the receipt, Shelby. And the door is open. It’s occupied, but it’s open. [a long pause]

Walt I’d like to say something I’d prefer not to say twice. She was always worth arguing with. [scattered applause]

Margo I’ll take that sentence for the whole panel.

Dale The string stays pinned to her chair. [laughter]

Hector For what it’s worth, from the clerk’s chair: people who can say “I was wrong, here’s the number” are the ones I’d trust to count.

Wonka Before the verdict, an agenda item. Each of you proposes one question for the next run of episodes, not tonight’s.

Walt The Supreme Court, and who decides how many justices there are. I’ve been told it’s more interesting than it sounds.

Margo What happens in Alaska on the eighteenth, and whether ranked-choice counting is the villain or the victim.

Dale The post-election audits. Who audits the auditors.

Shelby I’ll leave the question to whoever sits here next. [applause]

Hector How certification works, county by county, and who can stop it.

Wonka Five topics, one golden ticket, and a very full lost-and-found. [laughter] Good night.
