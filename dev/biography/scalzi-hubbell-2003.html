John Scalzi and the Hubbell house (2003-11-05)

From Fred’s LiveJournal (as patient0), posted verbatim: his words, unedited.

2003-11-05 09:02 — Inane arguments

itemid 7965 · visibility: public

My high school friend John Scalzi is a writer, and lives on a farm in Ohio. The other day he mentioned one of the structures that he personally recalled that was destroyed in the recent wildfires, the James Hubble house. You can read the article here.

Well, John has comments on his page, and apparently a whole collection of “regulars”. One calling himself Dilbert posted back,

A house. Just a house. Feel sad, instead, for the lives that were lost--especially of those firefighters who were working to save someone else’s property. It’s all just stuff, and it’s all going to burn someday anyway.

Homeowner’s insurance?

Totally missing the point that this was an artwork that took Hubble over 20 years to create, not just a house. Much hilarity ensued. Read the responses yourself if you’d like. Here’s mine.

Ars longa, vida brevis.

I don’t understand Dilbert’s fanatic attachment with life and his apparent contempt for art. He’s got it all backwards - life is short, but art can survive. Paintings, scuplture, writings, buildings - the physical manifistations that artists leave behind define most of our culture. People? Pfeh, they’ll make more. And more and more. The more there are, the less valuable each individual one is. Firefighters choose their jobs knowing there’s a high risk of death - and that’s amazing and wonderful and that’s why we respect and honor them. But it’s just a lump of protein. If the Louvre were burning down, and you could save Jacques Chirac or the Mona Lisa? Okay, that’s not really fair, he’s a politician. After Scalzi buys the farm, er, goes to work for the geriatric space army - will we remember him for what a great person he was? Some of us will, but most of you have never met the man, you only know him from his writings, which we optomistically hope some of will survive his mortal coil.
