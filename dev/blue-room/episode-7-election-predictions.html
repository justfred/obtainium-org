The Blue Room — Episode 7: The Day After, Our Panel Predicts

The Blue Room is a fictional late-night political talk show, written by Claude (an AI) as political satire. The host and guests are invented comic archetypes, not real people, and nothing a character says is the site author’s opinion. What is documented, disputed or unsupported is sorted at the end of each episode, with sources in the notes.

As of Sunday, November 1, 2026. The predictions are the characters’ guesses, made for fun, not forecasts and not voting advice. For how and where to vote, ask your county elections office.

Wonka Good evening, and welcome to the Blue Room. Tonight we make predictions, which is a polite way of being wrong in public. [applause]

  • Walt Dunning, the retired actuary, has priced the election and would like it noted that he charged for the hours.
  • Dale Truitt has a map of Tuesday and has marked the exits. [laughter]
  • Margo Bell has three versions of tomorrow’s headline and has circled the one she’s hoping to use.
  • Shelby Spinner, a red-hat regular, has brought an electoral map and a pen she refuses to cap. [laughter, a few groans]
  • And our guest chair, Hector Almeida, spent twenty years as a state elections director, where he learned that the most dangerous words in an election are “it’ll be quick.” [applause]

Wonka Tonight’s subject has four doors: Election Day, election night, the day after, and the next two years. Each guest opens one and makes a prediction, and we write it down so we can be embarrassed later. A few slides first.

1. Election Day. Tuesday, Nov 3. [1]

2. Election night. Pennsylvania and Wisconsin count late, with a blue-shift risk. Michigan pre-counts. Arizona and Nevada take days, California weeks, and Alaska about two weeks. [2]

3. The day after. County certification runs from Nov 9 (Georgia) through Dec 3 (California). Every county that has refused to certify since 2020 ended up certifying. [3]

4. The next two years. The new Congress convenes Jan 3, 2027. Democrats say they’d subpoena DOJ’s leadership on day one if they win the House. [4]

5. The odds. Senate: most likely 51–52 Democratic, Democrats favored about 60–75%. House: about 224–240 Democratic. [5]

Wonka Door one, Election Day, which is Wednesday—

Walt Tuesday.

Wonka I had a one-in-seven chance. [laughter] Walt, you open it.

Walt My prediction is a boring one, which is why I trust it. The biggest Election Day story will be small: long lines at a few places, and one county somewhere with a printer or equipment problem that gets national attention and turns out to be routine. It happens every cycle. If it doesn’t, I’ll buy the first round.

Dale I’m keeping an open mind about whether Tuesday will happen. [laughter] I’d like it noted that I had three theories about the date, and one of them was Wednesday.

Hector I’d put a footnote on Walt’s. “Routine” is the word I’d underline. Most of what makes the news on Election Day is a thing election offices have a form for.

Wonka Door two, election night. Margo, walk us through it.

Margo Okay. Step one: the first polls close in the East. Step two: Georgia, North Carolina, Texas and Florida count fast, and you’ll know a lot by midnight. Step three: Pennsylvania and Wisconsin count mail late, so the early picture can lean one way and shift. Step four: Michigan may not be called until Wednesday. Step five: Alaska’s ranked-choice count takes about two weeks. So my prediction, and it’s the least glamorous in the room: the Senate majority will not be known on election night if any close race is in the late-count group.

Shelby Margo, you’ve said “step” five times, and I’d like to say, as a mom, the people want to know on election night—

Margo The people want a lot of things, Shelby. [laughter]

Dale My prediction, and I’ll say it flat. Before midnight on Tuesday, a candidate in a close race will say the count is rigged. The surprise is who. I’m keeping an open mind that it’s the side that thinks it’s winning. [a few groans, a few cheers]

Hector That’s a safe bet in the sense that it’s happened before. And the thing I’d add is the dull part. A claim is not a count. The count is the only thing with a deadline.

Wonka New rule: if you say the count is rigged before it’s finished, you should have to say which count. [laughter]

Shelby And as a mom, I’d just like everyone to go to bed Tuesday night, be nice to the poll workers, and wait for the count.

Wonka That’s the most sensible thing said all evening, and I’ll mark it down as unscored. [a few cheers]

Wonka Door three, the day after. Shelby, you’ve been vibrating.

Shelby Republicans keep the Senate. And the Justice Department’s Alaska suit, the whole thing, gets its day. I’ll say it again, Republicans keep the Senate.

Wonka Noted, and entered. The models have Democrats favored for the Senate, about 60 to 75 percent.

Shelby The models have been wrong before, Wonka. Last year the off-year elections missed toward Republicans, by about nine points in New Jersey. [applause]

Wonka Yes, and. That’s real. The tracker has the 2025 off-year missing toward Republicans, and also notes that midterm polls were accurate to under a point in 2018 and 2022. The pro-Democratic misses were in presidential years. So the fair summary is: nobody knows, and a two-point miss would flip the Senate.

Shelby I’ll take “nobody knows” and put it on a mug. [laughter]

Hector My day-after prediction is the dullest of the night. Every state certifies on time. Two or three counties will delay, get ordered to certify, and certify. And the Alaska suit doesn’t change the certification timetable. I base it on this: every county certification refusal since 2020 ended in certification.

Dale Boring. I’d like the record to show I lodged an objection on behalf of excitement. I’m also keeping an open mind about whether a Senate majority is a state of mind.

Hector It’s not. It’s a spreadsheet with a deadline. [laughter]

Margo And I’d note, as a Democrat, that “favored” is a word that has gotten better people than me in trouble. [a few groans from the left]

Walt And the House isn’t the suspense. The tracker has it at about 224 to 240 Democratic, and Democrats favored at 90 percent plus, even with a 2016-size miss. The suspense is the Senate. If you want a number to put on a napkin, that’s it.

Wonka Door four, the next two years. Margo?

Margo If Democrats win the House, the first big move is subpoenas, not legislation. And here’s the surprise: Epstein is on the list, even though it isn’t on the first-hundred-days list. Rep. Garcia has said they’d subpoena DOJ’s leadership on day one. That’s a vow, not a plan, and I’ll be watching which one wins.

Dale I predict a shutdown fight by spring. I’d like it noted that this is a guess with a string on it. [laughter]

Wonka And my own prediction. I predict I will be wrong once tonight.

Walt You already were. It was Tuesday.

Wonka Then I’m done for the evening. [laughter, applause]
