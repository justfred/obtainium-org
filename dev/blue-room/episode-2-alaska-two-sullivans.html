The Blue Room — Episode 2: Alaska’s Two Sullivans

The Blue Room is a fictional late-night political talk show, written by Claude (an AI) as political satire. The host and guests are invented comic archetypes, not real people, and nothing a character says is the site author’s opinion. What is documented, disputed or unsupported is sorted at the end of each episode, with sources in the notes.

WONKA: Welcome back to the Blue Room, where I’m told the guest list has been rearranged. [applause]

  • Shelby Spinner is a proud MAGA mama bear. She has brought her own talking points and, I believe, a spare set for anyone who needs them. [laughter]
  • Margo Bell has been right about three elections and wrong about four, and framed all seven beautifully. [laughter]
  • Dale Truitt, formerly of overnight radio, believes tonight’s subject is connected to something larger, and has a map. He may be right. He may also be holding it upside down. [laughter]
  • Walt Dunning, retired actuary, has read the fine print on the studio lease and found it wanting. [a long pause] He has also read mine.

WONKA: Tonight’s subject comes in twos. [laughter] Alaska’s Senate ballot has Sen. Dan Sullivan, a Republican, and a second candidate, Daniel J. Sullivan, listed as unaffiliated. In the August primary, Peltola got 48%, the senator 43%, Daniel J. about 2%. The Justice Department sued October 22 to stop certification if the senator loses, citing voter confusion. Trump said he ordered it. A federal judge fast-tracked the case and no ruling had come as of Friday. And a New York Times report says Democrats helped put the second Sullivan there. I’ll be careful with that one. I’m told it’s the one that bites. Margo, you open tonight. Shelby has been spoken to, and has taken it very well. [laughter]

SHELBY: I took it beautifully, Wonka. As a mom, I just want to—

WONKA: Margo.

MARGO: Let me start with what the Times actually reports. The Times reports texts between Democratic Senate Campaign Committee aides and Peltola’s staff over months. Her former campaign manager, Elisa Rios, told the paper that Peltola wasn’t directly involved but was “definitely aware,” and then asked to retract. The campaign and the DSCC deny recruiting anyone. Peltola called it “offensive” and said she’d be “happy to fire” her former manager again.

SHELBY: Wonka, as a mom, this is simple. Democrats put a decoy on the ballot to trick Alaskans, the Justice Department is doing its duty, and—

WALT: Mm. Who says Democrats put him there?

SHELBY: —the New York Times says so, Walt, the New York Times, and I’d think you’d believe them. Decoy Dan. Decoy Dan.

DALE: Let me connect the dots, because they’re good ones. Months of texts. The same operative introduced to two senior staffers. A plan to create something called a “MAGA Party” that never happened. A manager who talks and then tries to take it back. If a Republican campaign had texts like this, you’d all be reading them aloud. [a few groans, a few cheers] Now here’s where the dots stop: none of those texts, as reported, is from Peltola. She’s not on the thread. The link to her is one former employee’s one word, “aware,” which she then tried to withdraw.

WONKA: That is the cleanest account of the evidence so far, and he’s the conspiracist. [laughter] Margo, where does the law stand?

MARGO: The state’s own Supreme Court ruled in June that both Sullivans could appear. State courts found nothing illegal in his candidacy, even if it wasn’t in good faith. And Gleason appeared skeptical of the timing of DOJ’s suit. The story I’d tell is the federal government asking a court to second-guess an election it hasn’t held—

WONKA: Margo. [beat] That is a framing. The facts: DOJ’s theory is a voter-registration law, and the state says it doesn’t reach this. The suit is conditional: it asks for action only if the senator loses. A judge may rule on dismissal before Tuesday.

SHELBY: The judge ought to listen to the Justice Department, because every Alaskan who voted for the wrong Dan Sullivan was cheated, and—

WONKA: Strike that, reverse it. Two minutes ago you said this was about the law. Now it’s about the voters. Which?

SHELBY: Both. Both, Wonka, it’s both.

[laughter]

WALT: Mm. Citation for “every Alaskan who voted for the wrong Dan Sullivan”? The second candidate got about two percent in the primary. I haven’t seen a survey of how many of those voters meant the other one.

WONKA: Nor have I. And here’s a mechanical point, which is mine, not a source’s. Alaska uses ranked-choice voting. A voter who mistakenly ranks Daniel J. first but ranks the senator second still has that vote go to the senator when Daniel J. is eliminated. Confusion only costs the senator votes for a voter who ranked nobody else. That doesn’t prove confusion is harmless. It does mean the harm is narrower than the headline.

DALE: Which brings up the ballot itself. The reporting says his line carries no modifiers, while the incumbent’s says “registered Republican” and “incumbent.” That’s the one place I’d say intent might matter. It’s not proof of anything.

WALT: Three more things I’d want. The Times texts themselves. The ballot-layout rules. And what the DOJ subpoenas, about three dozen to both campaigns, actually turn up.

WALT: One more, since nobody has asked it. Suppose the texts are exactly as reported. Is recruiting a long-shot candidate to split a vote illegal? As far as the reporting says, the state courts found nothing illegal in his candidacy. A thing can be sleazy, legal, and still worth a House investigation, and I’d want each of those three claims proven separately. What I don’t want is one word, “decoy,” doing all three jobs.

MARGO: And the DOJ wants to “segregate” ballots for Daniel J. Sullivan. The state’s attorneys called that “not even possible” because the ballots are commingled and it would break privacy. Both investigations, the House Oversight probe and the governor’s, are open. If I had to name the thing missing from this story, it’s a neutral referee.

SHELBY: Peltola lied, and—

WONKA: [gently] Which she denies, and one of her former aides said something different, and then took it back. Shelby, I’m sure you’ll be the first to say it needs a court.

SHELBY: The court will say what the American people already know.

[scattered applause, scattered groans]

Verdict

Documented:

  • Two Sullivans are on the ballot; the state Supreme Court ruled in June that both may appear.
  • DOJ sued Oct 22–23, Trump said he ordered it, and the suit is conditional on the senator losing. DOJ amended it Oct 29 and issued about three dozen subpoenas to both campaigns.
  • The state says segregating ballots is “not even possible” (privacy, commingled).
  • A federal judge fast-tracked dismissal and had not ruled as of Oct 31.
  • The Times reported texts between DSCC and Peltola-campaign staff, and that Rios said Peltola was “definitely aware” and asked to retract (via secondary reports; not read here).
  • Peltola and the DSCC deny recruiting; House Oversight and Gov. Dunleavy have opened probes.

Disputed:

  • Whether Peltola’s campaign or the DSCC recruited him (Times texts vs. denials).
  • Whether Peltola herself knew (one former aide’s word, then a retraction request).
  • Whether voter confusion would change any result, and how many voters it would affect.
  • Whether the ballot layout was an intentional tilt.

Unsupported:

  • That the DOJ suit has legal authority to block certification (the state, Peltola’s campaign and intervenors say no; no ruling yet).
  • That “every voter who picked the wrong Dan” was cheated: no survey of voter intent found.
  • That any text shows Peltola herself directing the plan (none reported).

Audience reactions

Three people from the audience, asked on the way out. Invented and representative.

  • A commercial fisherman, row 5: “The ranked-choice point was news to me. If I put the wrong Dan first, I’d still get a second choice. That changes how bad ‘confusion’ sounds.”
  • A county poll worker, on the aisle: “Everyone said ‘decoy.’ Nobody said what it would take to prove it. I’d have wanted that settled before the election, not after.”
  • A retired schoolteacher: “I’d read those texts just as harshly if they came from the other side. I’d also like to see the texts myself.”

Tag: Wonka, rising, to nobody in particular: “Two of them. And the one that’s real is the one on the ballot.” Then, more quietly: “Probably.”
