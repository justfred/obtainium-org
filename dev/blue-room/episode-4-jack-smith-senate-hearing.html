The Blue Room — Episode 4: The Jack Smith Hearing

The Blue Room is a fictional late-night political talk show, written by Claude (an AI) as political satire. The host and guests are invented comic archetypes, not real people, and nothing a character says is the site author’s opinion. What is documented, disputed or unsupported is sorted at the end of each episode, with sources in the notes.

WONKA: Good evening. I’m told there is a new device on the table. [he taps a small brass bell] It’s for grinding. I am not permitted to say more. [laughter]

  • Dale Truitt has been up since Tuesday with a corkboard and a very long piece of string. [laughter]
  • Walt Dunning is a retired actuary who has read the transcript, the other transcript, and the footnotes of the transcript. [a long pause]
  • Margo Bell has already drafted tomorrow’s chyron and would like the record to show she did it in pencil. [laughter]
  • Shelby Spinner, a proud MAGA mama bear, is on her best behavior tonight, which the producers have asked me to describe as “documented.” [laughter, groans]
  • Lorraine Voss, our guest chair, spent twenty years as counsel on a Senate committee. She wrote questions for senators, and tells us most were improved by being cut. [applause, then laughter]

WONKA: Tonight’s subject is a hearing. On Tuesday, September 29, former special counsel Jack Smith testified before the Senate Judiciary Committee, his second appearance before Congress. The hearing was titled “Oversight of Jack Smith’s Abuse of Authority and the Targeting of Republicans.” The central issue: during the Arctic Frost investigation, Smith’s team sought phone toll records for members of Congress. Chairman Grassley says a “wish list” named 14 members; other accounts say 16, and I don’t have a document that settles it. Smith says he wanted only who called whom, when and for how long, not content, and that the Justice Department’s Public Integrity Section approved. Dale, you open. Shelby, the bell is for you. [he does not ring it]

DALE: Thank you, Wonka. Dots. Fourteen or sixteen members of Congress on a wish list. Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile receiving at least eighty-four Arctic Frost subpoenas, per Grassley and Ron Johnson. Senator Blackburn suing over her records. [a few groans from the left, a few cheers from the right] And here is where the dots stop. Toll records are not content. And “approved by the Public Integrity Section” is Smith’s statement; I haven’t seen the approval.

SHELBY: Dale, that’s exactly what I’ve been saying, abuse of power, they went after Republicans, they went after sitting senators, and—

[Wonka rings the bell once. Soft. Polite. Final.]

SHELBY: —that’s a very nice bell, Wonka. [laughter] I’ll wait.

LORRAINE: Let me explain what a toll record is, because it’s half of this. It’s the call log: numbers, dates, times, durations. Not the call. A subpoena for it goes to the carrier, so the member isn’t the one served, and may learn later. Whether obtaining a member’s toll records raises a constitutional issue, I’d say that’s outside what I can speak to from this record. The practical questions a staffer asks are: who signed off, on what showing, and who was told.

WALT: Mm. Which brings me to who says what. Chairman Jordan’s House referral to the Justice Department says Smith lied at a previous hearing about the records. Smith’s lawyers dispute it. On top of that, an Oct 25–26 report from Just the News says emails cast doubt on his Senate testimony that his team followed filter-team protocols. One outlet, one set of emails I haven’t seen.

MARGO: And the hearing’s headline wasn’t any of that. Smith said his investigation proved, quote, “beyond reasonable doubt” that Trump committed “serious crimes.” Republicans called him a “villain” and a “dirtbag.” The story I’d tell is a hearing built to put a prosecutor on trial—

WONKA: Margo, that is a frame, and I’ll let you keep it for one sentence. [laughter] The thing everyone remembers is the basketball.

DALE: I want to say what happened. Senator Schmitt held up a poster of text messages from February 3, 2024, and suggested they showed Smith coordinating with Fulton County’s DA, Fani Willis, on game day. Smith said he wasn’t at that game. Smith had been at an Iowa–Maryland women’s college game. The senator, as far as anyone can tell, mixed up the Iowa Hawkeyes with the Atlanta Hawks. The texts, as The Hill reported, never named a team. Benny Johnson posted that Smith had been caught perjuring himself, and deleted it. Schmitt, per Fox News, refuses to apologize.

SHELBY: Senator Schmitt is a serious man, a serious senator, and The Hill is not exactly—

WONKA: Fox News reported the same thing, Shelby. [a few groans from the left]

SHELBY: Well, then Fox was being fair, Wonka, and I appreciate that. But the real story isn’t a basketball game. It’s the phone records of sitting senators, fourteen of them on a list, and a prosecutor who says it was all approved. Approved by whom? Name them. If it was approved, there’s a name on it, and if there’s a name on it, let’s see it. That’s all I’m asking, and I’d ask it of any prosecutor, of either party. [applause]

WONKA: That’s a fair question, and I’ll put it in the lost-and-found with the others. [laughter]

LORRAINE: And she’s not wrong that it’s a separate question. The basketball episode says nothing about whether the toll-record subpoenas were proper. It says something about whether a senator checked a claim before holding up a poster. Those are different claims, and the audience should hold them apart.

MARGO: And the framing problem belongs to everyone at this table. An oversight hearing and a gotcha shared one stage, and the people at home will remember whichever one made the better clip. That’s true for the senators who did well tonight, too, and I’d include my own side. [a few groans, then applause]

WALT: I’d add a number I wish someone had. How many of the 14 or 16 members’ records were actually obtained, as opposed to sought? Grassley says 14 on a wish list. I haven’t seen a count of what was delivered.

WONKA: Nor have I. That’s the first number tonight I’d put on the lost-and-found.

[scattered applause]

Verdict

Documented:

  • Jack Smith testified Sept 29 before Senate Judiciary at a hearing titled “Oversight of Jack Smith’s Abuse of Authority and the Targeting of Republicans and Related Matters.”
  • Smith said he sought toll data (who, when, how long), not content, and that the Public Integrity Section approved.
  • Grassley describes a “wish list” naming 14 members (other accounts say 16); Grassley and Sen. Ron Johnson say three carriers received at least 84 related subpoenas; Sen. Blackburn has sued over her records.
  • Rep. Jordan referred Smith to DOJ for alleged lying at an earlier hearing; Smith’s attorneys dispute it.
  • Sen. Schmitt’s Hawkeyes-for-Hawks mix-up: reported by The Hill, CNN, Fox News and others; Schmitt has not apologized.

Disputed:

  • Whether the toll-record subpoenas were an abuse of authority (Republicans: yes; Smith: approved, targeted at conspirators).
  • Whether Smith lied about the records (Jordan’s referral) or about filter-team protocols (one outlet’s emails).
  • How many members’ records were sought versus obtained (14 vs. 16 are reported figures; no delivered count seen).
  • Whether Smith’s investigation proved “beyond reasonable doubt” that Trump committed “serious crimes” (his statement; no trial).

Unsupported:

  • That the Feb 3, 2024 game-day texts show Smith coordinating with Fani Willis (the texts never named a team).
  • That Smith attended an Atlanta Hawks game. Reports say an Iowa–Maryland women’s college game.
  • A Florida connection: none found in any source.
