The Blue Room — Episode 3: Noncitizen Voting and California’s 20,000 Ballots

The Blue Room is a fictional late-night political talk show, written by Claude (an AI) as political satire. The host and guests are invented comic archetypes, not real people, and nothing a character says is the site author’s opinion. What is documented, disputed or unsupported is sorted at the end of each episode, with sources in the notes.

As of Sunday, November 1, 2026.

WONKA: Good evening, and welcome to the Blue Room. I’m told we have a full house and one extra chair, which is the most dangerous ratio in television. [applause]

  • Margo Bell frames the news for a living, and is rumored to have named at least one recession.
  • Walt Dunning is a retired actuary who believes a number is only as good as the person who counted it, and has met the person.
  • Shelby Spinner, a proud MAGA mama bear, has brought a binder. It has tabs. [applause, a few groans]
  • Dale Truitt spent his career on the overnight dial and has never once been asked what he knows about the daytime.
  • Ruth Ellery, tonight’s guest chair, spent thirty years as a county registrar of voters. She has told a great many strangers they were on a list, and a few that they weren’t, and was right about as often as the list was. [applause]

WONKA: Tonight’s subject is a list. A few slides first.

1. The suit. Oct 15: the Justice Department sues California over about 20,000 ballots it says come from “likely noncitizens.” [1]

2. The list. DOJ hasn’t published how it was built. California says the data is more than a year old. [1]

3. The number. About 190,000 flagged this summer. About 20,000 by fall. [4]

4. The ruling. Oct 28: a judge denies DOJ’s request. The 9th Circuit agrees, 2–1. DOJ appeals. [3]

5. The poll. 83% of Americans favor photo ID to vote. [7]

WONKA: Walt, you open. Shelby has promised to wait. She has it written on her hand.

SHELBY: [holding up her palm] It says “wait,” Wonka. And underneath it says “twenty thousand.” [laughter]

WALT: A federal prosecutor says every name was individually reviewed. Reviewed against what?

SHELBY: Twenty thousand noncitizens, verified one by one, and the California Democrats are fighting tooth and nail to keep those ballots counted. Every one an illegal vote.

RUTH: Every vote cast by a noncitizen would be illegal, and I’d enforce that. The question is whether these twenty thousand are noncitizens, and I haven’t seen anyone outside the government say so. Nobody has published how the list was built. What I know from thirty years of rolls is that the usual false positive is a naturalized citizen: someone who became a citizen after the file was made still looks like a noncitizen on an old file. Texas flagged about 100,000 in 2019, a federal court called it “a solution looking for a problem,” and many were naturalized citizens.

WONKA: In other words, the file thinks it’s still last year, which describes most files and several guests. [laughter]

DALE: This is one where I can’t make the string reach. Two readings of 190,000 down to 20,000. One: the checking worked. Two: the first list was bad and the second might be, too. I don’t have a thread for either. [a few groans, a few cheers] Reportedly, Los Angeles County called the list “unreliable,” and a sample suggested about one in four people on it appeared to be U.S.-born. That’s reported, not something I’ve seen.

WONKA: One word each. How many of the twenty thousand are noncitizens?

SHELBY: Twenty thousand.

DALE: Fewer than one hundred ninety thousand. [laughter]

MARGO: Whatever fits on the chyron.

WALT: Unknown. I’d like it noted that “unknown” is a number.

RUTH: I’d want to see the list.

WONKA: Five answers, four of them honest, and I won’t say which. [laughter]

MARGO: The story is that a number travels better than a footnote. “Twenty thousand” fits on a yard sign. “Under review” doesn’t. I once spent a week trying to rebrand a recall as a “spirited reorganization.” [laughter] And the judge’s remedy is the other half: even if every name were right, DOJ is free to prosecute individual noncitizens who voted, but setting ballots aside wholesale would “wreak havoc.”

SHELBY: My yard sign says “twenty thousand.”

MARGO: Mine says “under review,” and nobody’s bought one. [laughter]

SHELBY: Prosecute individuals! After the election, after the ballots are counted. Once a vote is in the count you can’t un-vote it. That’s the flaw in the judge’s answer, and nobody up here wants to say so.

WONKA: Yes, and. That’s a real point. The federal crime reaches the person, and a counted ballot can’t be pulled back. Whether that’s a flaw or a feature is the argument.

RUTH: And a ballot that’s set aside risks never being counted if the list is wrong. Whether there’s a middle path, a hold with notice to the voter, I can’t speak to from this record. It’s the right question.

WONKA: Ruth, if you ran it, what would you do first?

RUTH: Check every name against the county’s own rolls and tell the voter, before anything is set aside. That’s what a registrar does with any challenge. It’s slower than a press release.

WALT: On rarity. “Rare” and “non-problem” aren’t the same claim. Georgia’s Republican secretary of state audited about 8.2 million registrants in 2024 and found 20 noncitizens; 9 had voted. [5] DHS later said about 2,500. The AJC reported most were pending applicants, and only about 150 had previously voted. [6] Rare isn’t zero, and Shelby’s right that every one of those is illegal.

SHELBY: Thank you, Walt. Tab seven, Georgia.

WALT: Tab seven is the 2024 audit, which found twenty.

SHELBY: Twenty is a start.

WALT: Twenty is twenty.

RUTH: I’d just keep applicants, registrants and voters apart. A noncitizen who registers by mistake at the motor vehicle counter isn’t one who votes knowing it’s illegal.

SHELBY: And a September AJC piece found four hundred thirty potential noncitizens who voted at some point in thirty years, and California’s motor-voter program sent nearly twenty-three thousand erroneous registrations, and they want us to—

WONKA: The first is real, from SAVE and a state audit, and SAVE has misflagged citizens. “Potential,” “some point,” “thirty years.” Three hedges in one sentence, and I admire them. [laughter] The second is a headline I haven’t read. On photo ID, you’re on firmer ground: 95% of Republicans and about 71% of Democrats favor it. That isn’t a partisan position. [7]

SHELBY: Eighty-three percent! [applause]

MARGO: The same poll isn’t a poll on setting aside twenty thousand ballots from a list nobody has checked.

DALE: For the record, I’m still holding the string.

WALT: Is it attached to anything?

DALE: My pocket. [laughter]

Verdict

Documented:

  • DOJ sued Oct 15; a judge denied its emergency request Oct 28; the 9th Circuit rejected DOJ 2–1; DOJ is appealing. [1][3]
  • DOJ hasn’t published how the list was built. [1]
  • Georgia’s 2024 audit: 20 noncitizens of about 8.2 million registrants; 9 had voted. [5]
  • DHS’s Georgia flag of about 2,500: most were pending applicants, and about 150 had previously voted (separate from the audit’s nine). [6]
  • Pew: 83% favor photo ID; 95% of Republicans, about 71% of Democrats. [7]

Disputed:

  • Whether all 20,000 are noncitizens: a prosecutor says each was reviewed; no verification is published; an LA County sample reportedly suggests about 1 in 4 appeared U.S.-born. [2][4]
  • Why 190,000 became 20,000.
  • Whether prosecuting individuals afterward is an adequate remedy. [3]
  • The 430 “potential” noncitizens who voted over three decades (AJC; SAVE has misflagged citizens). [6]

Unsupported:

  • That all 20,000 voted illegally. The list is of ballots mailed, not confirmed votes.
  • That noncitizen votes have changed any known race.
  • California’s “23,000 erroneous registrations” (a headline I haven’t read). [8]

Audience reactions

Three people from the audience, asked on the way out. Invented and representative.

  • A woman who became a citizen in 2019, row 2: “I’d hate to find myself on a list like that. I’d want to know how it was made before I decided who to be angry at.”
  • A man in a ballcap, near the back: “I want ID. I want the list to be right too. Both can be true.”
  • A grad student, on the aisle: “You can’t un-vote a ballot. Somebody should answer that better.”

Tag: Wonka, to the empty chair: “A list is a lovely thing until it starts to talk back.”
