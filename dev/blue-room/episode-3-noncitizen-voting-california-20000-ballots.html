The Blue Room — Episode 3: Noncitizen Voting and California’s 20,000 Ballots

The Blue Room is a fictional late-night political talk show, written by Claude (an AI) as political satire. The host and guests are invented comic archetypes, not real people, and nothing a character says is the site author’s opinion. What is documented, disputed or unsupported is sorted at the end of each episode, with sources in the notes.

As of Sunday, November 1, 2026. Facts may have moved since taping; see “Since taping” at the end.

WONKA: Good evening, and welcome to the Blue Room. I’m told we have a full house and one extra chair, which is the most dangerous ratio in television. [applause]

  • Margo Bell frames the news for a living, and is rumored to have named at least one recession. [laughter]
  • Walt Dunning is a retired actuary who believes a number is only as good as the person who counted it, and has met the person.
  • Shelby Spinner, a proud MAGA mama bear, has brought a binder. It has tabs. [applause, a few groans]
  • Dale Truitt spent his career on the overnight dial and has never once been asked what he knows about the daytime.
  • Ruth Ellery, tonight’s guest chair, spent thirty years as a county registrar of voters. She has told a great many strangers they were on a list, and a few that they weren’t, and was right about as often as the list was. [a long pause, then applause]

WONKA: Tonight’s subject is a list, and the record is in the box below. The argument starts with Walt. Shelby has promised to wait. She has it written on her hand.

What’s on the record

  • Oct 15: the Justice Department sued California over about 20,000 mail ballots it says come from “likely noncitizens.” [1]
  • How the list was built: DOJ hasn’t published its method. Reporting says DHS ran California’s public voter list, obtained in August 2025, against its own files, and that the SAVE database wasn’t used for this list. California says that data is more than a year old. [1][4]
  • The size: the government first flagged about 190,000 people to California this summer, then about 20,000. [4]
  • The check: a federal prosecutor says every one of the 20,000 was individually reviewed by a federal employee. Nobody outside the government has published a verification. [2]
  • Oct 28: Judge Carter denied DOJ’s emergency request to set the ballots aside, finding DOJ likely lacks standing and a cause of action. A 9th Circuit panel then rejected DOJ’s bid 2–1, and DOJ is appealing. [3]
  • Photo ID, for scale: 83% of U.S. adults favor requiring it, including 95% of Republicans and about 71% of Democrats (Pew, Aug 4–10, 2025; 3,554 adults). [7]

SHELBY: [holding up her palm] It says “wait,” Wonka. And underneath it says “twenty thousand.” [laughter]

WONKA: Walt.

WALT: Before anything else. “Individually reviewed by a federal employee.” Reviewed against what?

SHELBY: Twenty thousand noncitizens, verified one by one, and the California Democrats are fighting tooth and nail to keep those ballots counted. Every one of them an illegal vote.

RUTH: Every vote cast by a noncitizen would be an illegal one. That’s the law, and I’d enforce it. The question is whether these twenty thousand are noncitizens, and I haven’t seen anyone outside the government say so. [2]

WONKA: Ruth has asked the question the evening turns on. What do we know about how the list was built?

RUTH: Not much that’s been published. I can’t speak to anything past what the reporting says. What I can tell you from thirty years of rolls is that the usual false positive is a naturalized citizen. Someone who became a citizen after the file was made still looks like a noncitizen on an old file. Texas flagged about 100,000 people in 2019, a federal court called it “a solution looking for a problem,” and many of them were naturalized citizens. [9]

DALE: I’ll be honest, this is a case where I can’t make the string reach. Two readings of 190,000 down to 20,000. One: the checking worked and the junk was filtered out. Two: the first list was bad and the second might be, too. I don’t have a thread for either. [a few groans, a few cheers] What I do have is that Los Angeles County’s preliminary analysis reportedly called the list “unreliable,” and a sample suggested about one in four people on it appeared to be U.S.-born. That’s reported, not something I’ve seen. [4]

WONKA: Dale, who has come without his string. Margo, the story?

MARGO: The story is that a number travels better than a footnote. “Twenty thousand” fits on a yard sign. “Under review” doesn’t. I once spent a week trying to rebrand a recall as a “spirited reorganization.” It’s not a profession for the honest. [laughter] The judge’s remedy is the other half: even if every name were right, he said DOJ is free to prosecute individual noncitizens who voted, and that setting ballots aside wholesale would “wreak havoc.” [3]

SHELBY: Prosecute individuals! After the election, after the ballots are counted, that’s my whole point. Once a vote is in the count you can’t un-vote it. That’s the flaw in the judge’s answer, Wonka, and nobody up here wants to say so.

WONKA: Yes, and. That is a real point. The federal crime reaches the person, and once a ballot is in the count it can’t be pulled back. Whether that’s a flaw or a feature is the argument. The judge’s answer was about the tool: DOJ can prosecute people, but it can’t override California’s processing. [3]

RUTH: And from my side of the desk, a ballot that’s set aside is a ballot at risk of never being counted if the list is wrong. Whether there’s a middle path, a hold with notice to the voter, I can’t speak to from this record. But it’s the right question to ask.

WALT: On how rare all this is, because “rare” and “non-problem” aren’t the same claim. Georgia’s Republican secretary of state audited about 8.2 million registrants in 2024 and found 20 noncitizens, 9 of whom had voted. [5] DHS later said it found about 2,500 in Georgia. The AJC reported most were pending applicants who never became registered voters, and only about 150 had previously voted. The nine is the 2024 audit, a different count. [6] Rare isn’t zero, and Shelby’s right that every one of those is illegal.

SHELBY: Thank you, Walt.

RUTH: The pattern I’d watch is counting applicants, registrants and voters as the same thing. A noncitizen who registers by mistake at the motor vehicle counter isn’t the same as one who casts a ballot knowing it’s illegal. Federal law has a “knowing” element for a reason.

SHELBY: And a September AJC piece found four hundred thirty potential noncitizens who voted at some point in thirty years, and California’s own motor-voter program sent nearly twenty-three thousand erroneous registrations, and they want us to—

WONKA: The first is real: it drew on SAVE and a state audit, and the same coverage notes SAVE has misflagged citizens. [6] “Potential,” “some point,” “thirty years.” Three hedges in one sentence, and I admire them. [laughter] The second is a headline I have in the record but haven’t read, so I’ll mark it unread. [8] Photo ID, though, you’re on firmer ground: Pew found 83% of Americans favor it, including 95% of Republicans and about 71% of Democrats. That isn’t a partisan position. [7]

SHELBY: Eighty-three percent! [applause]

MARGO: The same poll that backs ID isn’t a poll on whether to set aside twenty thousand ballots from a list nobody has checked.

Verdict

Documented:

  • DOJ sued California Oct 15 over about 20,000 ballots from people it calls “likely noncitizens.” [1]
  • Judge Carter denied the emergency request Oct 28; a 9th Circuit panel then rejected DOJ’s bid 2–1; DOJ is appealing. [3]
  • DOJ has not published how the list was built; California says the data is more than a year old. [1][4]
  • The government first flagged about 190,000, then about 20,000. [4]
  • Georgia’s 2024 audit: 20 noncitizens of about 8.2 million registrants, 9 of whom had voted. [5]
  • DHS’s Georgia flag of about 2,500: most were pending applicants, and only about 150 had previously voted (a different count from the 2024 audit’s nine). [6]
  • Pew: 83% favor photo ID (95% of Republicans, about 71% of Democrats). [7]

Disputed:

  • Whether all 20,000 are noncitizens: a federal prosecutor says each was individually reviewed; no independent verification is published. LA County’s preliminary analysis reportedly called the list “unreliable” and a sample suggested about 1 in 4 appeared U.S.-born (sources not opened; one D-aligned). [2][4]
  • Why the number fell from about 190,000 to 20,000 (filtering vs. a bad first list).
  • Whether post-election prosecution is an adequate remedy (Shelby: it can’t undo a counted vote; the court: DOJ can prosecute individuals but not override California’s processing). [3]
  • A September AJC follow-up counts about 430 potential noncitizens who voted at some point in three decades, from SAVE and a state audit; SAVE has misflagged citizens. [6]

Unsupported:

  • That every one of the 20,000 voted illegally (the list is of ballots mailed, not confirmed noncitizen votes, and no verification is published).
  • That the number of noncitizen votes has changed any known federal race.
  • California’s “nearly 23,000 erroneous motor-voter registrations”: a headline I haven’t read, so I can’t say what it counts. [8]

Audience reactions

Three people from the audience, asked on the way out. Invented and representative.

  • A woman who became a citizen in 2019, row 2: “I’d hate to find myself on a list like that. I’d also want to know how it was made before I decided who to be angry at.”
  • A man in a ballcap, near the back: “I want ID. I want the list to be right too. Both of those can be true.”
  • A grad student, on the aisle: “I keep thinking about the point that you can’t un-vote a ballot. Somebody should answer that better.”

Tag: Wonka, to the empty chair: “A list is a lovely thing until it starts to talk back.”
