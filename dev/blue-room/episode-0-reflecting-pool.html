The Blue Room — Episode 0: The Pilot (The Reflecting Pool)

The Blue Room is a fictional late-night political talk show, written by Claude (an AI) as political satire. The host and guests are invented comic archetypes, not real people, and nothing a character says is the site author’s opinion. What is documented, disputed or unsupported is sorted at the end of each episode, with sources in the notes.

Last serviced Friday, July 10, 2026

A legacy pilot: a proof-of-concept demo, written as if taped on July 10, 2026, using only what had been reported by then. The “Since taping” box at the end adds what happened next. Please ignore the furniture.

Wonka Good evening, and welcome to what I’m told is a pilot. [applause] That’s a television word for “we haven’t decided yet.” I’m Wonka. I’ll be hosting, unless someone objects.

  • Walt Dunning is a retired actuary who has read the contract and would like to speak to the person who wrote it. [laughter]
  • Margo Bell frames the news for a living and has brought three versions of the headline. [laughter]
  • Shelby Spinner, a proud MAGA mama bear, has brought a point of view. It is a very large point of view. [laughter, a few groans]
  • Dale Truitt sends his regrets. His string is in the mail. There is no guest chair this week; we’re told it’s still being assembled. [laughter]

Wonka Tonight’s subject is a pond. A few slides first.

1. The pool. The Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool reopened June 6 after a renovation reported at more than $16 million. The bottom was painted blue. [1]

2. The contract. A no-bid award to Atlantic Industrial Coatings, first $13.1 million, later $14.6 million. The company had no prior federal construction experience. [1]

3. The problems. The new coating began peeling within about two weeks, and the water turned green. [1]

4. The claim. On June 21 President Trump blamed vandals; the Interior secretary said the cuts in the coating were made by vandals and that the administration has “video and eyewitness reports” it hasn’t released. [1]

5. The case. David Hearn, 67, a former Olympic canoeist, was indicted July 2 on one felony count of destruction of property. He pleaded not guilty Thursday. [2]

Wonka Margo, you open.

Margo Okay. Here’s the order of events, because the order is the story. Step one: in April, a $1.7 million no-bid contract for a “nanobubbler” to treat the algae, to a company that had never used it on a pool. Step two: in May, a nonprofit sues over the plan to paint the pool blue. Step three: June 6, the pool reopens. Step four: within about two weeks, the coating’s peeling. Step five: June 21, the president blames vandals. Step six: July 2, an indictment. That is six steps, one of which is a person.

Shelby Margo, as a mom, I’d like to say the old pool was green for years, and these people fixed the green, and everyone—

Walt It was green again within weeks, per the reporting.

Shelby —and everyone’s very upset about a pond, when there are people who vandalize national monuments. Seven arrests, Walt. Seven.

Wonka The administration says seven arrests. That’s in the record. What I can’t tell you is what they were for.

Walt Numbers, then. The president first put the renovation at $1.8 million. Reporting now says more than $16 million. That’s a ninefold gap. The contract was $13.1 million, then $14.6 million, about an 11 percent increase. And the Times reports the bid included a 20 percent profit margin, against 6 to 12 percent typical for federal contractors. Twenty is double the high end. I’d like the person who wrote that bid in the studio.

Shelby Walt, businesses make a profit. That’s called America.

Walt It is. It’s also called a denominator. [laughter]

Margo I’d add that a firm that worked on an earlier renovation reportedly passed on this one as “unfeasible,” because of the July 4 deadline and the blue-bottom requirement. [3]

Wonka Yes, and, let’s take the human part, because there’s a human in it. A man is charged. He says he reached into the water and touched a strip of peeling lining on a bike ride. Prosecutors say he ripped up part of the pool floor with his bare hands, and that he told staff “It’s not your pool,” which is an allegation. His lawyers call the indictment “outrageous.” Whether it was peeling before he touched it is the whole case.

Shelby And if he did it, he should be held accountable, as a mom, I say that about anybody. Vandals are real, Wonka. I’d say the same if it were a Democrat’s pond.

Wonka That’s fair, and I’ll note you said “if.” [applause]

Walt I’d say the prosecution’s best evidence is the thing they haven’t shown: “video and eyewitness reports.” If they exist, they’re a short walk from a courtroom.

Margo And the interior secretary said on Sunday that the same company will do the repairs, because “they did a fantastic job.” Those are all real words in a row. I’ll let you sit with them. [laughter]

Wonka And that’s the first rule of this show, which I’ve just made up: nobody leaves without a ledger. [laughter]

Verdict (as of July 10)

Documented:

  • The pool reopened June 6 after a renovation reported at more than $16 million. [1]
  • Atlantic Industrial Coatings won a no-bid contract, $13.1 million rising to $14.6 million; the firm had no prior federal construction experience. [1]
  • The coating began peeling within about two weeks and algae turned the water green. [1]
  • Hearn was indicted July 2 on one felony count and pleaded not guilty July 9; he says he only touched a peeling strip. [2]
  • The administration says it arrested seven people over alleged vandalism. [1]

Disputed:

  • Whether vandalism or the installation caused the peeling.
  • The renovation’s cost (Trump’s early figure of $1.8 million against the reported $16 million or more).
  • Whether Hearn tore up the lining or touched lining that was already peeling.

Unsupported (as of July 10):

  • That vandals caused the damage. The administration says it has “video and eyewitness reports”; none had been released.
  • That Hearn “forcefully and violently” ripped up the floor. That is the prosecution’s allegation, and he denies it.

Since taping

Taped July 10; updated Nov 1. In late July the Justice Department moved to dismiss the case, acknowledging the damage “was the result of flawed installation by the contractor.” On August 6, Judge Todd Edelman granted the request in part and set a schedule on whether the dismissal should be with prejudice. On October 1 he dismissed it with prejudice, citing the risk that political pressure could revive the prosecution. [4]
