Camping and Bodie

August 2026 · Age 12

In early August, Dylan went camping in the Sierra Nevada mountains with Papa and Aunt Amy.

The trip started at a grocery store. There was a picture of Aunt Amy leaning over a full shopping cart to hug Dylan, in an aisle with birthday balloons.

At camp, there was a creek and a lot of trees. Dylan sat by a big tree with his knees up and looked back at the camera. He found a log jam, a pile of fallen trees across the water, and he climbed over the logs. He crossed one log with his hand held out to keep his balance. He sat cross-legged on another one. The creek had a rusty-orange color, from the minerals in the rocks.

Dylan helped set up the tent. He hammered the tent stakes into the ground with a rubber mallet. Aunt Amy leaned in to help him on one stake. In the next picture, Dylan is hammering a stake all by himself.

Dylan sat around the campsite in a tie-dye bucket hat, in a camp chair, with a can of Squirt and a straw. He poked at the kindling in the fire ring. He stood by the creek with both hands up, clapping, while the campfire glowed in front of him. Aunt Amy cooked at the camp stove. She cooked a tortilla, and she smiled with Dylan for a picture. Dylan ate a cup of instant noodles with a fork at the picnic table.

One day, they drove to Bodie. Bodie is an old gold-mining town that people left long ago. The buildings are still standing, and some lean over a lot. Dylan took his stuffed lemur with him. He held it up on the porch of the old general store. He stood in front of a leaning wooden building and jumped. He stood on a wooden beam by a huge rusty mining gear, and reached out to touch its giant teeth. He peeked through windows, and stood in the doorway of an old building, grinning.

At the Methodist church, Papa, Dylan and Aunt Amy stood together on the steps for a picture. Later they stopped at an overlook of Mono Lake. Dylan, in a blue camp shirt and a pink bandana, leaned his head on Aunt Amy’s shoulder.
