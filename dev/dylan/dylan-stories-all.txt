# How Dora Got Here

*February 2010 · Four years before Dylan was born*

Before Dylan was born, before Papa and Mama had any kids at all, they were looking for a dog. Their first dog. They did not have one yet, and they wanted one very much.

Dora's story starts a long way from the ocean. She started out in El Centro, a flat, hot city in the desert near the Mexican border. That is the same town where Grandma Elise and Grandpa Phil lived. Dora was a stray. That means she did not have a family. She was a dog on her own, and nobody knew exactly when she was born. The best guess was the fall of 2008, so she was a young dog.

Somehow Dora made her way from the desert to a shelter in Encinitas. A shelter is a place where dogs and cats wait for a new home. Encinitas is a beach town in San Diego County. It has more trees than El Centro, and a lot more fog, and the ocean is close by.

Mama worked at that shelter. Every day she was around dogs who needed homes. One of them was Dora.

Mama found her there. When Mama came home, she told Papa about the dog. There is a video from the time Dora joined the family. On it, you can hear Mama's voice. She says, "Come here, Dora. You are so precious." She says, "We are looking for our first dog."

That was February 18, 2010. It is the day Dora came home with Papa and Mama.

Dora did not stay a stray. From then on, she had a family and a house. She had Papa and Mama, and she had the house's cats and, later, a parrot named Pasquale. She had long walks and many trips to the beach.

Dora was a small dog. When she was about four years old, she weighed around seventeen pounds. Small, but she was a lot of dog.

A little less than four years after that day, Dylan was born. Dora was already part of the family when he came home. She had been there first.

So that is how Dora got here. She came from the desert, to a shelter by the sea, and then to a home where somebody had been looking for exactly a dog like her.

# Dora at the Beach

*2012–2013 · Before Dylan was born*

Before Dylan was born, Dora and Papa went on a lot of walks. Many of them were near the ocean.

Dora was a small dog, about seventeen pounds. But she loved to move. Papa liked to take her places where she could stretch her legs.

One place was Oceanside Harbor. It is a harbor full of boats. On one Sunday in December 2012, Papa took Dora there for a long, good walk around the whole place. Then they walked out along the breakwater. A breakwater is a long wall of big rocks that sticks out into the water. It keeps the waves from crashing into the boats.

Another place was Carlsbad. Papa wrote about taking Dora for a walk there in June 2013. Dogs are not allowed on that beach, so they did not walk on the sand. They walked near it.

Her favorite place might have been Fiesta Island. Fiesta Island is in Mission Bay, in San Diego. Papa wrote that when they went there, Dora got to run around and chase other dogs. That is one of the things dogs like best. There is room to run, and plenty of other dogs to run after. Papa wrote about walking "the dogs" there in February 2013, and about a quick beach walk where Dora ran and chased dogs.

Papa liked those walks too. One day at Fiesta Island, in the spring of 2013, Papa was walking Dora by the water. He wrote that he thought his life was perfect. He wrote that it felt like a perfect moment. He thought that if the next day was his last, he would have had a really good life. He said that getting Dora had been great, and that having her meant a lot to him.

That is a nice thing to think while walking a dog. It does not mean everything in life was easy that day. It means that for a little while, Papa was exactly where he wanted to be. He was by the water, on a nice day, with a good dog.

Dora was a good dog. Dylan came along a little later, and Dora was there for him too.

# Pasquale

*Spring 2013 · Before Dylan was born*

Before Dylan was born, a parrot came to live at the house. The parrot's name was Pasquale.

Pasquale was a yellow-naped Amazon parrot. That is a green parrot with a patch of yellow on the back of its neck. Pasquale was fifteen years old, which is a lot of years for most animals, but parrots can live a very long time.

Pasquale came from the neighbors next door. They had kept Pasquale for a while, and now they were passing Pasquale on to Papa and Mama. Mama worked at a bird hospital, so she knew a lot about birds and had help learning more. Papa and Mama said they were about to become parrot parents.

At the neighbors' house, Pasquale had been living outside, in a homemade aviary. An aviary is a big cage that a bird can walk and climb around in. So Pasquale was already used to having a lot of room. Papa and Mama had to figure out how to make a new space with some shade. It also needed to keep Pasquale safe from animals, small ones and big ones.

First they found a used aviary from a bird breeder. It was a big box made of chain-link fence. They went and picked it up. Papa's car was a Land Rover, and it carried five big sections of chain-link on its roof. Inside it carried about ten long, thick deck boards, some other lumber, and half a dozen concrete blocks. Those were for a small raised deck in the yard to set the aviary on.

Papa also built a roof for the parrot cage, and a rain roof for the back porch. He wrote about that busy weekend of building.

They put the aviary among the trees in the yard. They hoped it would be far enough from the house so the noise would not bother the person who rented a part of the property.

They also got a secondhand cage for inside the house. That way Pasquale could get used to the cats and the dog. Pasquale could talk, had been hand-fed, and also made loud beeping noises.

Pasquale liked loquats. That is a small orange fruit that grows on trees.

When Dylan came along, Pasquale was already there.

Kindergarten is the first year of real school. It was a new start for Dylan, and a new schedule for the whole house.

School starts early in the morning. To get Dylan ready and out the door on time, everyone in the house had to be up at six o'clock. That is very early, especially in the summer. Papa had to get up when he hadn't before.

So every school day began the same way. The alarm went off. Dylan got up and got dressed. He had breakfast. Then he went to school.

Papa wrote about it in his journal about two weeks after school started. He said it was a busy and crazy time. Dylan had started kindergarten, and they had to get up at six to get him ready.

That is how kindergarten started for Dylan. Alarm, breakfast, out the door. Every day.

The puppies were not staying forever. They were on loan. That means the family was taking care of them for a while, and then they would go back.

Papa wrote about them in his journal. He wrote what puppies do all day long. They pee. They poop. They eat. They sleep. They play. Then they do it all over again. That is a lot for three little puppies to fit into one day.

But puppies are also soft and warm. They have that puppy smell, which is one of the best smells there is.

And Dylan loved them. Papa wrote it down in just a few words: "Dylan loves them."

There were three of them, and Dylan loved them all.

# The Hike

*November 2019 · Dylan, age 5 · Dora, age 11*

In the fall of 2019, Dylan was five years old. He had a dog named Dora, who lived with the family. Dora had joined the family four years before Dylan was born, so she was a grown-up dog, and by this time she was getting older.

One day in November, Papa decided to take Dora on a hike. This was a hike for just the two of them. Dylan was not on this one.

They walked for about two hours. That is a long time to walk. Papa wrote it down in his journal that night. He said it was more walking than either of them had done in a long time.

Think about that. Papa had not walked that much in a long time. And Dora had not walked that much in a long time, either. Two hours is a lot of steps for a man, and a lot of steps for a dog with older legs.

But they did it. Papa and Dora walked the whole way together, side by side, and they made it home.

Dora was a good walking buddy. She liked beaches and she liked walks, and she had walked with Papa for many years already. She kept living with the family for more than four years after that hike.

This was just one ordinary day in the long friendship of one man and one dog. Nothing big happened on the hike. Nobody got lost. Nobody found a treasure. They just walked for two hours, and then they went home.

Sometimes that is a good enough story.

# Mission Bay

*January 9, 2021 · Age 7*

January 9 is Dylan's birthday. In 2021, he turned seven.

It was a Saturday. Papa wrote in his journal that day: "Dylan's birthday, Mission Bay."

Mission Bay is a big, wide bay in San Diego, with water, beaches, and grassy parks along the edge. The family went there to celebrate. It was a good place for a birthday party, with lots of room for people to be together.

Dylan did not celebrate alone. Many people from his family came to Mission Bay that day.

Auntie Amy came. Amy is Papa's sister. Her daughters came too, Dylan's cousins Chloe and Lydia.

Two more people came, too: Cindy and Mark. Cindy is Mama's mother, so she is Dylan's grandmother. Mark is Cindy's husband.

That makes a lot of people for one small boy's birthday. Dylan had his aunt, his two cousins, and his grandmother and her husband, all together at the water with Papa.

Seven is a good age to be. By seven, a kid can run fast, tell what he likes, and pick his favorite people out of a crowd. Dylan had a whole group of favorites there on the shore.

Papa did not write down what they ate, or what presents Dylan opened, or what the bay looked like that day. He only wrote down who was there and where. But Papa kept that line, and now it is part of the story of Dylan growing up.

The day was a Saturday. The place was Mission Bay. The people were the ones who love Dylan. And Dylan was seven.

That is the story of the birthday.

# Two Teeth

*January 2021 · Age 7*

Dylan turned seven on January 9, 2021. A few days later, something big happened to his smile.

On Wednesday, January 13, Dylan had both of his two front teeth pulled. That is the top middle two, the ones everybody sees first when you grin. Papa wrote it in his journal that day: "Dylan's two front teeth pulled."

Losing one tooth is a big deal. Losing both front teeth on the same day is a really big deal. When it was over, Dylan had a new gap right in the middle of his smile.

Now, a lost tooth means one thing: the tooth fairy. Everybody knows how it works. You give up a tooth, and the tooth fairy leaves you something in its place.

The next day, Thursday, January 14, Papa wrote one more line in his journal: "Tooth fairy five dollars."

So the tooth fairy came, and she did not forget. Dylan had given up two teeth, and the tooth fairy left five dollars.

Five dollars is a good amount for a seven-year-old. It is more than a few candy bars. It is enough to feel like a real prize.

Papa also noted that this was the same week as his work's big release, the one called R13. It was a busy week for Papa. But he still stopped to write down the teeth and the tooth fairy. Those were the things he wanted to remember.

Dylan's grown-up teeth would come in later, to fill that gap. But for a little while, Dylan had a brand new smile, with a space in the middle of it, and five dollars to go with it.

That is the story of the two teeth.

# Bird Rock

Tide pools are little pools of ocean water left behind in the rocks when the tide goes out.

That is what Dylan looked like at Bird Rock. He was careful, he was smiling, and he had a small yellow toy along for the trip.

In the notes Papa kept in 2021, Mission Bay and the Bird Rock tide pools were places the family went again and again.

So this was one Saturday out of many. It had a bay, and rocks, and tide pools, and a photo of a seven-year-old with a toy in his hand and a smile on his face.

# Sabio

*September 2022 · Age 8*

Sabio is a tortoise. He is a little one, and he lives with Dylan's family. He was one of Mama's pets, and Dylan has grown up with him in the house and yard.

Sabio gets taken outside, and brought back in, and when it is cold he gets his heat turned on. Mama and Papa trade those jobs back and forth. He is a pet who needs a little care every day.

In September 2022, Dylan was eight years old. That month, Sabio had a big adventure.

On Monday night, Papa came home. It was hot, so the doors were kept closed to hold in the cool air. Papa brought Sabio inside and tucked him in a towel.

In the morning, Papa went to take Sabio out. But Sabio was gone.

Sabio had wandered off, which he sometimes does. You might think it is easy to find a tortoise in a couple of rooms. But Sabio is very good at hiding. Papa looked in the afternoon, and then all week the family kept looking. They moved boxes. They looked under the couches and under the desks and in the cabinets. The doors had been closed the whole time, so nobody knew how he could have left. Papa wrote that Dylan did not say he had moved him.

A week went by. Then, a week later, on Monday, Mama got a phone call. Someone had picked up a tortoise on Gopher Canyon Road. That road is about a mile from the house. The tortoise was not hurt. Papa guessed that cars on the road had probably gone around him, and not the other way around.

It was Sabio.

How did he get there? Papa thought he must have gone out the back, where farm roads run behind the yard. Then he went on, through fences and cactus hedges, and across strawberry and pumpkin fields, up hills and down. Those are very small legs for a very long trip. And in the end, he came out safe.

Sabio had an adventure, and he lived through it. Papa said the family would put his tracker back on, so they could find him next time. It would even help them find him in the house.

# Youtopia

*June 2023 · Age 9*

In June 2023, Dylan was nine years old, and he went camping with Papa at a festival called Youtopia.

Youtopia is the San Diego area's own Burning Man event. Burning Man is a giant gathering in the desert where people camp together, make art, and dance. Youtopia is a smaller version closer to home. It was as close as Dylan had come to Burning Man.

Papa got ready for the trip on Thursday, June 8. He wanted to get there not too late. He planned to make carne asada to bring.

# Telluride and Ouray

# The Creek

*June 2023 · Age 9*

In the mountain towns of Colorado, little creeks run almost everywhere. They are fed by melting snow from higher up, so the water is quick and cold and so clear that you can see every pebble on the bottom. It runs fast in the narrow parts and slows down where it spreads out.

Dylan and Papa were on a road trip through those mountains. They had been driving for days. There had been a hot springs pool, a big bridge over a canyon, and a black bear that Papa saw by the road while Dylan slept in the back seat. On this day they were in the town of Telluride. Near a park in the middle of town, there was a creek just like the ones in the stories. Dylan found it.

He was not doing anything complicated. He found some sticks and leaves and set them in the water at the top of a little current. Then he watched them travel. The current carried them off. So he got more sticks. He tried a bigger leaf. He tried a shorter stick. Dylan stood there for a long time, watching each one go, while Papa sat on a bench nearby and watched Dylan.

That day, a message went from Papa's phone to Mama. It said, "Hi mom mom I found a stick."

A little farther on there was a bigger stream, wide and fast. Papa noticed that the water was only about 36 degrees, so cold that his feet would go numb in about a minute. People had brought their dogs there. The dogs leaped in after balls and sticks, swam out into the current, and swam back, dripping and happy, over and over. Dylan watched the dogs the same way he had watched his sticks. He was interested, and he was in no hurry at all.

Papa liked the big stream best. He told Grandma Elise that he did. Then he wrote that Dylan liked floating leaves down the little one. They each had a favorite. Papa took lots of photos of the water that week. Later, Grandma Elise looked at them and said she loved all the flowing water.

That week had bigger things in it. There were hot springs, long drives through the mountains, a train ride with Grammy and Gramps, and the alpine slide that Dylan loved. But there was nothing big about this part. Dylan stood at the edge of a small creek in Colorado, trying out leaves and watching to see how far they would go.

# Dora

*March 2024 · Dora, age 15½*

Dora was a stray. That means she didn't have a family, and nobody knew exactly when she was born. The best guess is the fall of 2008.

She started out in El Centro. El Centro is a flat, dry desert city, a long way from the ocean. It is also where Grandma Elise and Grandpa Phil lived. Somehow Dora ended up at a shelter in Encinitas, a beach town with more trees and more fog than the desert.

Mama was working at that shelter. One day she came home and told Papa about a dog. That was February 18, 2010. After that, Dora's home was with Mama and Papa.

Papa took her to Oceanside Harbor, and they walked out along the breakwater, past the boats. They walked on Carlsbad Beach. They went to Fiesta Island in Mission Bay, a big open place with paths and grass and other dogs. Dora loved to run there. She loved chasing other dogs, which is one of the things dogs like best.

Papa wrote once that he was at Fiesta Island, walking Dora, and he thought his life was perfect. He did not mean that everything was sorted out. He meant he was exactly where he wanted to be, with a good dog, on a nice day.

Then Dylan came along, in January 2014. Suddenly there was a baby in the house. Dora got used to it. The baby turned into a boy, and Dylan grew up with a dog. That is a lucky thing. It is hard to notice that kind of luck while you are in the middle of it. In 2017, when Dylan was about three, there is a picture of him with Mama, Papa, and Dora in the wildflowers at Borrego.

In December 2023, the vet found that Dora had lymphoma. That is a kind of cancer. It meant she didn't have much time left.

Papa's birthday is December 13th. That year he turned fifty-eight, and he took part of the day off work. He wrote that he wanted to take Dora for a walk. He wanted to give her some time outside, in the air, while she still could. He didn't make a big deal of it. He just took the dog for a walk. That is one quiet way of showing love.

Dora died on March 8, 2024, at home, with Mama and Papa beside her. She was about fifteen and a half, which is a very long life for a dog.

She had a good life, and a family that loved her.

# The Kern River Rafting Campout

*May 2026 · Age 12*

On Memorial Day weekend, Dylan and Papa went camping with the Boy Scouts. It was a rafting campout at the Kern River. Dylan had wanted to go camping, and this was a real Scout campout.

Papa had packed everything the night before. They had to get up at 4:30 in the morning. It was a long drive. Papa likes the drive through the desert. On the way, he drives right past a giant Joshua tree that he once took a picture of.

Papa brought the iPad in case he wanted to write in his journal. There were a lot of Scouts on the trip, and some grown-ups too. Jocelyn and Chris were two of the grown-ups. Phoenix was one of the Scouts, and he helped load the tent.

The Scouts did some organized activities, and Dylan was part of the best one. He went rafting, and he did great. He paddled with the rest of the boat. He hung on through the rapids. And when the water calmed down, he jumped out of the raft and swam. The Scouts also went swimming, and they practiced using an axe and starting a fire. Chris made sure Dylan got to join in the axe practice and the fire-starting practice. Papa wrote down that these activities went well.

The Scouts did the cooking themselves, in big heavy pots called Dutch ovens. One night the boys decided to make chicken pot pie. They bought pie crusts that were already shaped, and those did not work for a pot pie. They forgot to buy chicken. So Jocelyn went out and bought two roast chickens. The boys did not know how to take a chicken apart. They chopped it up with a big knife, bones and all, and tried to pick out some meat. In the end, the pot pie was not good to eat. Papa wrote that a five-minute lesson in cutting up a chicken would have helped a lot.

Papa also washed dishes. Jocelyn showed him how, in about two minutes. Then Papa did the dishes three or four times.

Between activities, Dylan wanted to play with his tablet and his toys, and he wanted Papa to hang out with him. Dylan did not always understand why Papa could not.

When they got home on Monday, Papa was very tired. He wrote that it had been a long weekend of camping and water play. Dylan had fun, he said, though Dylan would have liked it best to just hang out with Papa.

Papa made a joke about it. He said that if this were a movie like *Forrest Gump*, Dylan would be the one who started the fire and saved the whole troop. Dylan paddled, held on through the rapids, and swam when the water was calm, so he was not far off.

# Papa

*2026 · Age 12*

Every day, Papa asks Dylan about his day. On weekends he asks twice.

He asks what Dylan did at school. He asks what sports he played and what he had for lunch. He asks what songs he is singing. He asks who his favorite friends and teachers were, and who dropped him off, and who picked him up. Papa asks about math and reading, too. These days it is mostly math.

Some of Papa's questions are silly. He might ask what Dylan thinks about the weather, or whether spaghetti is better than pizza, or the other way around.

Dylan knows when a question is silly. When Papa asks one, Dylan tilts his head to one side and says one word: "Papa!"

That one word says a lot. It says he knows Papa is being ridiculous. It says he finds this both tiresome and funny. It says he has no plan to stop the conversation. Often he follows it with the real answer. Sometimes the real answer is "I don't know." Papa asks anyway.

Dylan has a good sense of humor, especially when Papa teases him a little or makes a joke.

At night, Dylan still wants a story before bed. He has wanted this for as long as Papa can remember. He likes it best when Papa reads out loud, though he is fine with a story from memory too. Papa reads to him every night. Stick Fluffy, his toy, is usually there for the stories. Dylan brings Stick Fluffy with him when he goes places, the way he always has.

Dylan and Papa also build with Legos, and they talk about their day and their dreams.

After school and on weekends, Dylan dances. He has strong opinions about music, especially a group called KPOP Demon Hunters. He doesn't just listen to the music. He moves to it, with full commitment. The dancing is serious, and it is also joyful.

Dylan is twelve now. He is getting taller all the time. He is turning into a teenager. He knows when a question is silly, and he knows when a story is a good one. He knows which toys come along and which stay home.

Papa still asks the questions anyway.

# Youtopia, Again

*October 2026 · Age 12*

In October 2026, Dylan was twelve years old. Three years after his first Youtopia, he wanted to go again. Youtopia is the San Diego area's own Burning Man event, a festival where people camp together, make art, and dance.

Papa's back was still hurting. But Dylan wanted to go, so they went.

First they had to set up the tent. Then Papa found out he had been sent to the wrong place. After dinner, Dylan and Papa dressed warm. They walked around the festival at night, from about six o'clock until midnight. Papa brought a small chair to sit on.

Late in the night, they ran into their friends Brady and Becca. The four of them went on a long walk around the top of camp. They saw lots of cool art and met some cool people. Papa wrote that they had good conversations.

Finally they went to bed, around 12:30. The tent was windy and flappy, and Papa did not sleep well.

The next day, more wind was coming. Papa took the tent down again and put it away. They went on a walk. There was not a lot to do during the day, so they decided to leave.

But they did not go straight home. Instead, they went to visit Grandma Elise. Dylan got to swim in her pool.

That was Youtopia, again. Papa wrote that they had a good day. Dylan wanted to go, and Dylan got to go.
