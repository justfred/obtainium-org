Flashlights and Chicken Tacos

July 2025 · Age 11

One weekend in July, when Dylan was eleven, Papa and Mama were clearing out the garage. It was a big job. A shed had to come down. A fridge and a big desk had to go. People came to pick things up, and Papa sold or gave away chairs, a featherbed and a lot of other stuff.

In the middle of all that, Papa and Dylan went out “secondhanding.” That means they went to look at used things. Papa found some Hydroflasks and some bottle caps. Dylan got flashlights.

Papa had a list for the day. It had breakfast at Silverback on it, and SkyZone, and a trip to Sprouts, the grocery store. After Sprouts, Papa wrote the best line of the list. He wrote: “Taught Dylan to take apart chicken and make tacos.”

That is a real skill. A chicken has to be pulled apart, and some people do not know how. Papa taught Dylan. Then they made tacos with it.

The next day was a Monday. Papa dropped Dylan off at “camp,” and then went to work with sore muscles from all that lifting. At camp, the kids were going to the beach. The camp asked if Dylan could swim. Papa did not have Dylan’s swimsuit with him, so he said it was OK for Dylan to swim in his shorts. And Mama planned to pick him up at the beach, so that Dylan could go to his swim lesson.

That night, Papa left some chicken and rice in a bowl for Dylan’s dinner, in case he wanted it. But Mama had already picked up dinner. When they got home, Dylan was already hungry.

So there was a flashlight, a taco, a swim in shorts, and a very hungry boy.
