The Card Game — Rules

October 20, 2026

No board. No app. No winners. (Well, maybe one winner.)

The Basics

Draw cards. Tell a story. Play each card as you use it.

That’s it. Everything below is details.

How to Play

  1. Shuffle the deck. Someone draws first — pass the deck clockwise after each turn.
  2. Draw 3–5 cards. Look at them. Take a breath.
  3. Tell a story that uses your cards. First, second, or third person — your call.
    • “I woke up next to a mermaid and a bottle of mezcal…”
    • “You’re at a funeral. Nobody’s crying. You notice something in the casket…”
    • “There was a man who believed he was always right. He wasn’t.”
  4. As you use a card, play it face-down. That’s your signal to the group: this one counts.
  5. The group votes instantly — thumbs up or thumbs down. No debate, no deliberation. Majority rules.
  6. Your score = number of cards the group accepted.

Wild Cards

Some cards are images, some are prompts, some are weird — and some stack both. They all work the same way: the card is your muse, not a rule to satisfy. You don’t have to use it literally.

“If you could live on any island, where would you live?” becomes “They lived in a small fragrant apartment above a creole restaurant and bar in Martinique…”

Some wild cards have a bonus layer — a cryptic phrase, a hidden instruction, an accent to attempt, or centuries of symbolic meaning if you happen to know it. Use any of it if it adds something. Skip it if it kills the story.

“Death. A change was coming — the end of an era, the start of a new adventure.”

Either way, putting the “wild” in wild cards is the point.

Variants

Draw & Discuss — No story needed. Draw one card, say what it means for your life right now. Pass the deck.

Character Profile — Draw 3. They’re not story beats; they’re personality traits. Build a person. Give them a name.

Versus — Two players each draw 3, tell competing stories. Group picks the better one.

Round Robin — Each player draws 1–3 cards and adds their beat to a shared story. Play passes clockwise. Audience votes each card as it’s played down. The story belongs to everyone — and so does the mess.

Tarot Spread — Draw 4, lay them out:

  • Past / Present / Future / Hidden influence
  • or: Who you were / Who you are / Who you’re becoming / Wild card

The One Rule That Matters

If a card feels like a stretch, the group will let you know. Own it anyway. The best stories come from impossible cards.

Special Cards

Some cards come with their own twist — it’ll say so on the card (or the dealer will tell you). Follow it if you want. Ignore it if the story’s already good.

Example: an accent card might require at least one line delivered in that accent.

Blank Cards

They’re in the deck on purpose. Write something. Play it. Leave it in.
