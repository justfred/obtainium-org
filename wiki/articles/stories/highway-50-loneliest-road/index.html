The Loneliest Road, and Anubis

From photo batch review, May 2026. Photos from the 1990 move from Denver to San Francisco.

The photos are from the drive: Highway 50, called the Loneliest Road in America, from Denver west to Nevada and then into California. I was moving from Denver to San Francisco with everything that would fit in the car.

One night along the way I pulled into a “motel” outside some small Nevada town — four mobile homes corralled together into a loose quadrangle. The night manager was shirtless and lifting weights. I was bleary-eyed and exhausted. I paid, showered, slept in an uncomfortable bed, woke up the next morning and drove to Fallon.

In Fallon I went to a Denny’s for breakfast. I came in, sat down, and waited. And waited. Nobody came.

I started to think: I must have died in the night. The man with the weights in the dark parking lot was Anubis. The highway was the path where I’d spend eternity. The Denny’s was — something. The in-between.

Then the waitress came over, brought water but no coffee and no menu, and I realized it was just bad service.

The SF arrival photo shows the Land Rover with the blue tarp still strapped on the roof, Victorian houses on the street behind. The other end of the same road. Anubis was somewhere in the middle, and I’d made it through.
