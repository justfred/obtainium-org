Stingray Sting

Southern California coast, sometime in the 2010s.

I was wading out through chest-high water, shuffling my feet like you’re supposed to. The shuffle is the correct technique — it scares the rays off the bottom before you step on them. I was doing it right.

A small wave picked me up. When I came back down, a ray had swum under my foot. The barb got me. It hurt like hell — one of those hot, immediate, deep hurts that doesn’t leave any ambiguity about what just happened.

The lifeguards used very hot water to ease the toxin. That’s the correct treatment. No stitches required.

I’m more careful now. I always start swimming once the water’s high enough to keep my feet off the bottom. But a wave can still pick you up and set you down on something you didn’t put there. I could easily get stung again.

The rays don’t want to sting you. They’re just down there, minding their own business, and then suddenly a large mammal lands on them. I understand the impulse.
