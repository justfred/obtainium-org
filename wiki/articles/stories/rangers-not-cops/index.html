We're Not Cops, We're Not Your Mom

The core mandate of the Black Rock Rangers.

The simplest summary of what Black Rock Rangers do is also the motto: “We’re not cops, we’re not your mom.”

What this means in practice: if you’re having an experience, the Ranger’s job is to hold space for you to have it. Not to judge it, redirect it, improve it, fix it, or take credit for helping with it. The Ranger is present. The Ranger is available. The Ranger does not prescribe.

This is harder than it sounds. Most people, when they see someone struggling, want to do something — offer advice, call for help, make the situation better in some visible way. The Ranger training is partly about learning to stay present without doing that. The hardest part is trusting that presence itself is the intervention, and that a person who is not alone is usually better off than a person who is alone, regardless of what the person who’s present actually says or does.

The Green Dot skill formalizes this: you quietly sit with someone who’s in a difficult experience. No fixing. No credit. You’re just there.

I had a Green Dot shift in 2022 with no “customers” — meaning no one came to us with a crisis. I noted this without disappointment. The shift that nobody needs is still worth doing. The availability is the thing.

Rangers are volunteers. The gift is invisible labor. We’re not cops. We’re not your mom. We’re just here.
