India's Oven Burns

India’s Oven, on Pico near Fairfax, Los Angeles›?. Circa 1992.

India’s Oven burned down during the Rodney King riots in 1992. They served great Indian food on paper plates because they had no room for an industrial dishwasher.

If you really wanted it hot, you’d tell the waiter, and he’d say “No no no, you don’t want it that hot!” And if you actually did — your ears would ring with every bite, you’d sweat down the back of your neck, you’d have the most incredibly vivid dreams that night, and an uncomfortable time the next day.

The waiter’s “No no no” was not performative. It was practical advice from a man who had watched this scene play out before. He knew you didn’t want it that hot. He also knew that if you persisted, you’d get exactly what you asked for, with full consequences.

They’ve since moved and rebuilt. Something was different about the original — the paper plates, the cramped room, the lack of pretense. The heat itself was probably the same. But the setting shaped the experience, and the setting is gone.

The riots burned a lot of places that didn’t come back the same way. India’s Oven came back at all, which puts it ahead of many. But you can’t rebuild the specific combination of paper plates and a waiter who warns you honestly about the vindaloo.
