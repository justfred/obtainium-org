Death Valley for Thanksgiving

Thanksgiving 2020. Nine months into lockdown; the first real escape.

After nine months at home — garoffice, Zoom calls, Dylan in and out of the frame, the whole 2020 domestic arrangement — Thanksgiving became a road trip. Death Valley, Valley of Fire, Oatman, Hoover Dam. Photos shared November 29.

Death Valley in late November is not the Death Valley of August. The salt flats are cold. Zabriskie Point in the low winter light is different from the bleached midday version. The National Park is largely empty; people who aren’t local don’t think of it as a fall destination, which makes it excellent.

Valley of Fire in Nevada is vivid red sandstone against desert sky. Oatman is a Route 66 mining ghost town in Arizona with feral burros that walk the main street and expect to be fed. Hoover Dam is an engineering miracle that looks like the future imagined in 1936.

Dylan at six: interested in the burros, capable of walking reasonable distances, good in the car. The landscape tolerated him and vice versa.

This was the escape valve. Eight months of working from the garage and Zooming and not going anywhere, and then three days of open desert and family and a different horizon every morning. The photos from this trip have a different quality than the earlier 2020 photos — more space, more distance, less of the domestic foreground.

It was enough to hold until the world opened again.
