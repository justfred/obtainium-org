The Tioga Pass Brakes

~1975. Family road trip over Tioga Pass in a large Class A motorhome.

We made it up to the top of Tioga Pass just fine, then headed down. Dad is one of the best drivers I know — he was being careful, mindful of the brakes. But about two-thirds of the way down, they started smoking. That nasty burning-asbestos smell. The RV was going faster than he would have liked, and the brakes weren’t responding well.

As soon as there was somewhere to pull off, he did — which was more dramatic than he would have liked, given the speed. We finally got stopped.

My mom LEAPED out of the RV. My sister and I claim we still have footprints on our backs where she climbed out over us. Then she turned around to make sure we were okay.

Dad used the fire extinguisher. We waited for the brakes to cool. Soaked our feet in the nearby stream. It was a memorable part of the trip.

Dad made a lot of good decisions in a compressed amount of time. I thought about that later, when I started learning to drive.
