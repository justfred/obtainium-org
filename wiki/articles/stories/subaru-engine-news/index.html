The Subaru Engine News

November 22, 2017. Rod knock.

Journal entry, November 22: “rod knock.” Diagnosis from the shop: approximately $4,000 for a short block repair.

November 30: new quote. Engine replacement, $6,000. Transmission while they’re in there, $1,500. Total: $7,500. Still owe $3,000 on the car. Trade-in value: $3,000 to $5,000. A new car would require a $3,000 down payment and about $400 a month.

The math: repair or replace? The math said repair was marginally better than financing a new vehicle, assuming the rest of the car stayed together. I wrote “cheaper than a new car, but…” in the journal and left the “but” floating there.

Decision: take it to the Subie Shop on Monday. Engine replacement.

The Subaru had been a source of accumulated trouble for a couple of years by this point. The rod knock was not a surprise to a car that had been communicating its intentions in less direct ways for some time. A car with a rod knock is telling you, clearly and loudly, exactly what it needs. In that sense it was more honest than the previous months of ambiguous symptoms.

I paid the $6,000. The Subaru kept running for a while longer, through alternator failure and mice and a brake failure in 2018, accumulating more stories.
