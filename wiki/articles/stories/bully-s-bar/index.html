Bully's East

Bully’s East Prime Bistro, San Diego.

When I have an evening to myself, I like to sit at the “Low Bar” — a big round table at the end of the bar where you can drink and dine. It’s more social than a regular table and it’s fine to sit there by yourself and chat up the other losers.

As a protein devotee I like the prime rib — some of the best I’ve had in town, though I’m a cheapskate and don’t go to fancy places that often — and the side asparagus (extra charge). Creamy horseradish, loaded on. Beer on the side because I’m not having carbs otherwise.

The Low Bar is a specific San Diego institution: the circular communal high table at the end of a proper bar, where you show up alone without it being strange. You can eat dinner, have a beer, talk to whoever’s next to you or not. It’s a social space without social pressure.

I’ve been coming here for years. It stays the same. The prime rib stays the same. The bar stays the same. Some places earn their reputation and then maintain it by not changing, and that’s sufficient justification for continued patronage.
