Gram and Gramp's Ashes at Mount Signal

From photo batch review, May 2026. Two photos: Mount Signal at golden hour; the trip to scatter the ashes.

The photo DSC_0464 is a desert landscape at golden hour: Mount Signal in the distance, tilted stones in the foreground. The stones are Gram›? and Gramp›?'s tombstones. The ashes were scattered there — illegally. The desert permits this, regardless of official policy.

Mount Signal — Cerro Centinela in Mexico — was their old picnic spot. They used to come there on horses. The marker is no longer accessible because of the border wall.

There’s a later photo from the actual scattering trip: the crew standing at the site, the rock slabs described as tombstones. Fred›? knew the spot from family stories. He knew what this place had meant to them before it meant something else.

The family buried them where they’d wanted to be, in a desert landscape on the border, in the place they’d come to on horses when the country was different. The border wall closed off access to the marker later.

Gram was Marjorie Meyer Heald. There’s a separate photo from around 2020 — black and white, at the Dana Harbor Inn — of Gram in a cloth bucket hat, warm sharp-eyed smile, turned toward the camera. Fred’s caption: “She was really starting to fade at that point.”

The ashes went to the desert. The photo went in the archive.
