Frisbeetarianism: Disc Golf at Burning Man

September 2003. Burning Man.

My Burning Man project that year: Frisbeetarian Disc Golf. Inspired by this year’s theme, “Beyond Belief,” and George Carlin’s statement that “Frisbeetarianism is the belief that when you die your soul goes up on the roof.” And of course the fact that people who play disc golf regularly tend to consider it a religion.

I hand-built two not-quite-regulation disc golf nets — Hole 1 and Hole 19 — and two tees. Decorated Hole 1 with electroluminescent wire (that never really worked right) and Hole 19 with propane flames (that the wind was too strong to run — c’est l’merde). Then I had Innova print up 100 golf discs with the Frisbeetarian logo (Burning Man figure with a disc golf net for a head) and the motto, “What goes around comes around.”

F’n expensive — and they were really snotty about it. “We’re not sure we want to sell these to you if you’re going to give them away.” But they looked awesome and people liked them.

I got placement way, way out on the playa for Hole 1, which was cool with me. I managed to give away almost all the discs; never did get the tees set up or even see the signs made. The EL driver sucked, and there was too much wind to ever light the propane. But they looked cool.

Maybe next year.
