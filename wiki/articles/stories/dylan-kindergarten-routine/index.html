Dylan Starts Kindergarten

September 2019. The new schedule begins.

“Dylan started kindergarten two weeks ago; we have to get up at 0600 to get him ready.”

That’s the entry. Dylan had been eligible for school services for years — Down syndrome qualifies children for early intervention and special education — but kindergarten was the start of the full school schedule. Up at 0600. Dressed, fed, on the bus.

He was five, going on six. He had the energy he’s always had — the kind that doesn’t slow down when asked, that covers ground at a pace that requires the adults to keep moving to keep up. Getting a five-year-old with Down syndrome ready for a school bus at 0600 is a specific kind of morning.

What the journal doesn’t capture: the school was good for him. He consistently did well when the environment was structured and he was engaged. The adults around him who worked with him tended to find him rewarding to teach. He knew what STOP meant; he occasionally chose to ignore it; but the structure worked.

The 0600 wakeup became part of the household rhythm. Some things you adjust to because you have to; some you adjust to because they’re actually fine once you’re in them. The kindergarten routine was both.
