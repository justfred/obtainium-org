IFR Lesson by Accident (The Blog Version)

March 2, 2005, Santa Monica›? area. Flying in actual clouds, unplanned.

Had my first “real” IFR lesson today, accidentally — though with my instructor.

We had planned to fly out to Cable Airport and then down to Long Beach, but the weather didn’t hold up so we went to Torrance instead. Got on the runway fine in VFR, but by the time we’d taxied back it was drizzling and closing in. Jesse — my instructor — filed a Tower Enroute IFR clearance, and I got to fly it back to Santa Monica.

Clearance: fly runway heading (250) to 800 feet, turn to 270 for vectors to Santa Monica, climb and maintain 3000, expect 4000 ten minutes out. Which is what we flew — heading out over LAX, then back east to the VOR approach to SMO. We went in and out of clouds most of the way, dropping out to VFR about four miles east of the airport.

I didn’t do the radio work for the IFR portion. Just flew the plane, which was challenging enough. I’ve flown with a “hood” — a visor that blocks outside reference so you’re flying instruments — but never in actual clouds. Actual IFR might be somewhat easier; I get claustrophobic with the blinder on. In clouds, at least you can see something out the window, even if it’s just grey.

The GPS track from the flight was, of course, immediately saved. I love having the trace.

Starting IFR ground school the following week. Written test to follow, then instrument approaches. After that I won’t be quite so worried about this sort of weather.
