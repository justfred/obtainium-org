Tiki Oasis, Finally

July 30, 2021, San Diego›?. Tiki Oasis, year twenty, first attendance.

Tiki Oasis had been running for twenty years and I’d never been. Finally went.

Solo outing. Wore a tropical caftan. The people-watching was good. I didn’t stay for the evening music.

Tiki culture is a specific kind of mid-century American fantasy about the South Pacific — rum drinks with garnishes, tikis carved by people who had varying relationships to the actual Polynesian cultures being cited, bamboo and rope and the general atmosphere of a vacation from the ordinary. It’s kitsch elevated to art form, which is a description that also fits quite a bit of Burning Man aesthetics.

The event has two distinct audiences: people who are deeply into the culture and the history (the actual tiki bars of the 1950s, Don the Beachcomber, Trader Vic’s, the specific provenance of drinks and carvings), and people who just like the aesthetic and the rum. There’s room for both.

The caftan was correct for the setting. People-watching at Tiki Oasis is its own reward: everyone has a look, and the looks range from authentic vintage to maximally committed tropical drag to “I found a Hawaiian shirt this morning.” All of it is right.

I didn’t stay for the evening music. But I’ll go back.
