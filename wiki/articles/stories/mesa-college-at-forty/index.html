Back to School at Forty

Early 2004. Mesa College, San Diego›?. First time in school in sixteen years.

I enrolled in Java, C/C++, and Systems Analysis classes at Mesa College in early 2004. This was the first time I’d been in school in sixteen years.

I was about 39. The other students in Systems Analysis were substantially younger. One of them, Erin, was pregnant and also recruiting me for a mortgage company. One of them, Marie-Pierre, had recently been in a wedding and I wasn’t sure whether she’d heard about my fashion preferences. The class had the slightly surreal quality that night school always has: adults with jobs sitting in a room together, processing information they will actually use, which is different from regular college in ways that are hard to articulate but immediately obvious.

The Systems Analysis class was mostly things I already knew, framed in vocabulary I recognized. The Java class was more work. I was not the fastest student but I was paying attention in a way that I probably hadn’t been the first time around.

I went back because I wanted the credentials and because I’d realized that sixteen years of self-teaching had given me a practical foundation that was very wide and not always deep. There were things I did by instinct that I wanted to understand properly.

The other students were polite about the forty-year-old in the room. I was polite back. We all passed.
