Alligator in Louisiana

January 1999. De Ridder, Louisiana. A work install for Applied Materials.

The install in De Ridder was straightforward enough. The memorable part was dinner at Catfish Junction, where I tried alligator.

Alligator is chewy. I’m not sure what I expected — I think I expected it to taste like chicken, which is what people say about unusual meats when they want to make them sound approachable. It tastes like alligator. It’s chewy. It comes out of a Cajun kitchen tasting like something that’s been in a dark water for a long time and isn’t apologetic about it.

I ended up with fried shrimp instead, which was excellent. The crawfish were too much work and too messy. I joked about Cajun cuisine with the women there, which went over approximately as well as jokes about local food always go over — politely, with a slight adjustment to the smile.

De Ridder is in a dry parish, which I didn’t know until I tried to find a beer. “Two down, one to go,” someone said, which was a WWII reference I didn’t catch until later.

I had tickets for the whole weekend in San Francisco›? before the Louisiana trip. I regretted going. Then the install was done and I was in De Ridder eating alligator, and I regretted it differently — not for going, but for the way travel always makes you realize you preferred the place you just left until you arrived at the next place.

I wanted to photograph a hovercraft near Houston on the drive back. It got dark too fast.
