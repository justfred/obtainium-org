The Sailing Final Exam

Catalina Island Boys Camp, approximately age 14.

I was on the sailing “fraternity” at camp, taking my solo sailing final exam.

The instructor was a “monkey” — his job was to go around the boat causing things to fail. Dropped halyard. Released lines. Tiller overboard. You had to figure out how to deal with each one. The point was to simulate the things that actually happen at sea, where there’s no one to help you.

One exercise was “lost the drain plug.” He’d pull it out, and you had to find something to plug the hole. The deck was clear and shipshape. Nothing available.

I stuck my toe in, which worked. He disallowed it. “You can’t keep sailing that way!”

So I had to take off my bathing suit to plug the hole, and sail back naked.

At least I wasn’t close to shore.

The certification meant something. You’d actually had to solve a series of actual problems with whatever was available, under conditions the instructor had deliberately made adverse. A plug made from a bathing suit is less elegant than a plug made from purpose-built equipment, but it accomplished the same thing. The boat didn’t fill with water. That was the test.

I passed.
