Kip's Week in Dallas

September 3, 1996. Kip writes on his LA office letterhead from Dallas. He’s annotated the margin by hand: “The stuff dreams are made of.”

"You should have taken the bet — to wit, that I would not still be living in Dallas three years hence. In fact, you should have asked for disproportionately higher odds, contingent upon how early I would bail out. You could have cleaned up.

I lasted exactly a week at the Yale Law Offices before succumbing to absolutely the worst nervous breakdown of my life. It was so severe that the agony, physical and psychic, virtually came full circle to ecstasy. By the middle of last week, a stroke or heart attack was imminent.

And afterwards, my dear fellow, I became more depressed than you and Mansbridge at your worst, combined — contemplating self-murder based not upon nihilism but sheer despair.

I’m incommunicado for now — not only sans e-mail, fax, but even basic telephone service. And the lights will be cut Friday, so candles it will be (though no wine, woman or song). As the Fretalians might say, C’est la dolce vita.

So, as you have probably ascertained, I’m coming back, and soon. Let’s get together and see if we can put our company-loving misery to good use. Look for me later this month."

He signed it Kip. No last name. The letterhead already said everything.

I had apparently told him he wouldn’t last. I wish I hadn’t been right about that one.
