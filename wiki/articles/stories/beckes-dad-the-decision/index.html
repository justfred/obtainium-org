Becke's Dad: The Decision

January 30, 2007. Daily Grill, Downtown LA. Happy hour menu, $2.95 a plate, all you can eat. Walking home in the rain after.

Becke may need to let her dad die. Her sister needs to know if it’s a 30% chance of getting better — less pain? I’m not exactly sure what the Torah says about the 30% rule. In any case, they need to decide: do a bypass operation in his leg, or cut off the leg, or stop going to dialysis and let him die. And then they may have to decide whether to file a wrongful death lawsuit.

Which seems like a really nasty thing to do. What would we be trying to do with that? The lawyers say it’s payback for the time lost with her dad, but money can’t ever pay that off. Does that mean you should or should not ask for money? Is it going to teach anyone a lesson? Is it going to penalize the insurance company?

Thing is, if George had had better care he might not have had this ongoing problem with his leg. He wouldn’t have been able to avoid dialysis, but he might have been able to put it off for a while, and he might have had a hell of a lot better quality of life the past few months, as would Becke. She’s worried about getting $4000 together to pay for his babysitters. It’s hard to separate the personal rationalizations from the equitable justice.

Some days my brain just runs over with ideas. Other days, it’s all I can do to stay awake.
