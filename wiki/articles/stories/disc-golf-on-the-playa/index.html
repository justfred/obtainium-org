Disc Golf on the Playa

Burning Man 2003: Beyond Belief.

For the 2003 burn I built a disc golf course for the playa. Frisbeetarian themed. Holes 1 and 19, plus tees. A hundred custom-printed golf discs with the Frisbeetarian motto — “What goes around comes around” — printed on them.

The propane-flame markers were too windy for the playa. The electroluminescent wire didn’t work the way I’d planned. I didn’t set up the tees as originally designed.

I gave away almost all of the discs. People were happy to receive them. Some of them had never thrown a disc before. Some of them threw them across the playa and didn’t come back.

“What goes around comes around” is a disc golf phrase and also, broadly, a philosophy. On the playa, where you bring gifts and give them away and expect nothing in return, it fits particularly well. The discs were never meant to come back.

The art project that doesn’t fully execute is a Burning Man staple. You have the vision, you build the thing, you bring it out, and the playa teaches you which parts of your plan were contingent on conditions that don’t exist on an alkaline flat in the Nevada desert. The wind was real. The electroluminescent wire was not suited to the environment. The discs worked fine.

I kept a few. The rest are out there somewhere.
