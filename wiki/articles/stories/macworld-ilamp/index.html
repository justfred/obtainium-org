The iLamp

January 8, 2002. Macworld Expo announcement.

The new iMac announced at Macworld 2002 was the “iLamp” — which is what people called it before Apple gave it the official name. Hemispherical white base. Articulated arm. 15-inch LCD floating at whatever angle you wanted.

I watched the announcement from my home office and wrote about it in my journal. The floating display was right. The arm was clever. The base contained everything — the computer, the power supply, the drive — in a half-sphere that sat on the desk and generated almost no footprint.

I’m an Apple person. I have been since the early 1980s. The 2002 iMac was the year Apple committed fully to the flat display — the CRT era was over — and the design language was unmistakable. The arm is the kind of engineering decision that looks obvious afterward: the display needs to be at eye level, the base needs to be stable, the cord management should be invisible. If you solve all three constraints, you get approximately that design.

I didn’t buy that particular iMac. I was already juggling too many computers. But I watched the announcement carefully, as I watch all of them, and wrote down what it meant.

Predicting technology turns is something I’ve been trying to do since I was a teenager reading about the personal computer revolution from El Centro›?. Sometimes I’m right. I thought someone would bring back the International Scout twenty-five years before Scout Motors announced it. I also thought the Pinzgauer›? would get a mass-market reissue, and that hasn’t happened yet.
