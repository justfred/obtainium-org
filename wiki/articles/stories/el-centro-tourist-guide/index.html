El Centro: A Tourist Guide

I grew up in El Centro, from 1965 through about 1980. So when someone asked how a tourist could cover all the attractions in a week, I had some thoughts.

The first day, compare Mexican food at Sobe’s, Letty’s, La Fonda and Camacho’s. Get a special quesadilla at least once, and some local beef.

The second day, take a trip to nearby Mexicali, have some Chinese food, get your teeth fixed cheap, grab some booze (I suggest Herradura tequila) and some tourist junk, wait in a long, long line to get back.

On day three, visit the local agricultural fields. Take a walk out and listen to the asparagus grow. Watch an agricultural burn. Hire on to pick lettuce and tomatoes. Smell the feedlots where cattle is fattened on local alfalfa. Go on a horseback ride.

Day four, if you’re there at the right time, go to the MidWinter Fair. Be sure to catch the livestock auctions.

Day five, cruise the old downtown.

On day six drive up to “the Slabs” and see Salvation Mountain and the Salton Sea. Marvel that this used to be a major Hollywood getaway area.

Finally, on day seven, drive out to Mt. Signal. Enjoy the desert peace and quiet, say hi to the border patrol, collect some rocks and fossils, see the border monument. Eat at Camacho’s again on the way home.

The major downsides of living there: it’s a small town, it’s hot in the summer (up to 120°F, occasionally more), and there’s high unemployment. But the weather is pleasant much of the year, you get to know your neighbors, you are close to fresh produce, there are lots of great Mexican restaurants, and you are close to Mexico — specifically Mexicali, a major metropolitan area with lots of entertainment, restaurants, and culture.

Nearly all houses and businesses are well air conditioned. Many houses have swimming pools. Real estate is relatively inexpensive, by California standards.
