First Solo Flight

From photo batch review, May 2026. El Monte Airport, early 1990s.

El Monte Airport. Rental Piper PA-28, tail number N5480F. Me, with a beard.

“This is me, can you believe it, when I first solo’d.”

The incredulity is still audible in the caption, written decades later. It’s not false modesty — it’s the genuine surprise that this happened. That I was the person who did this. That on a particular morning at El Monte Airport, the instructor got out of the plane, and I took off by myself, and flew the pattern, and landed, and did it again.

A first solo is one of the things that can’t be explained to someone who hasn’t done it. You’ve had someone in the right seat the whole time, and then you don’t. The plane feels different — lighter, higher, more responsive — because the weight is gone and now everything is just you. The radio is still there; ATC is still there; but you’re making every decision, and nobody is going to reach for the controls.

I landed. I took off again. I landed again. At some point the instructor walked back out and it was done.

The photo: a young man with a beard next to a Piper, with the expression of someone who can’t quite believe what just happened. About right.
