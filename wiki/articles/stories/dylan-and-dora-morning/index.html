Dylan and Dora in the Morning

May 2018, Vista›?. Morning routine.

The daily rhythm: get up, feed Dylan, handle breakfast, walk Dora›?, intercept the cats when they’re carrying lizards in. By 2018 Dylan was four and the house had been organized around his schedule for a while.

The cats — Tikka›? and Masala›? — had opinions about what should come indoors. Usually these were correct opinions (themselves, at mealtime), sometimes less correct (lizards, still alive, as gifts). Intercepting them at the door before the lizard became a domestic incident was a regular skill.

Dora the dog was a constant through the whole period — beach walks, disc golf, the yard. She was the kind of dog who adapts to the household’s schedule and accommodates whoever’s in it. Dylan and Dora coexisted on the mutually agreeable basis that each found the other interesting and neither made unreasonable demands.

“Dylan’s doing a great job putting up with the little boy,” the journal notes at one point about one of the cats. The categories had gotten fluid. The household was a system with multiple dependent components, and the morning routine was the daily calibration.

Getting everyone fed, watered, and out the door was the first problem of every day. It usually resolved.
