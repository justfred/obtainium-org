Authors I Keep Coming Back To

Top shelf, no argument: John Scalzi and Michael Crichton. Scalzi writes smart, funny science fiction that doesn’t take itself too seriously — The Kaiju Preservation Society gave me the most pure reading enjoyment I’ve had in years. Crichton never stopped being interesting, even when the science was off.

Elmore Leonard is right there with them. Nobody writes dialogue like that.

Below that tier but never disappointing: Tom Clancy, Neal Stephenson, Anthony Bourdain, Kurt Vonnegut, Ray Bradbury. Stephenson I’ll keep reading even when he buries me in pages. Bourdain I read like he talked — fast, sideways, suddenly personal.

The Bobiverse books (Dennis E. Taylor) hooked me and I binged all five. Same with the Culture series (Iain M. Banks) — I’m working through those now.

The Three-Body Problem: read it, watched the Netflix adaptation, rated both the same. Three stars. Great premise. I’ll read the trilogy anyway.

Hunter S. Thompson I’ve been reading since I was a teenager and I’m still not done with him. Currently in Freak Kingdom, a biography. He keeps finding new angles.

Things I keep rereading: Dune, Brave New World, Cryptonomicon. Things I’ve read but people look at me sideways for not having finished: The Brothers Karamazov. I got there.

For non-fiction: anything involving historical obsession or survival — Midnight in Chernobyl, The River of Doubt, Unbroken, American Prometheus. Oppenheimer remains the most interesting story about the moment science became terrifying.
