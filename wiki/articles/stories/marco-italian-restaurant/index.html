Marco Italian Restaurant

Marco Italian Restaurant, Escondido.

This place feels like the good old Italian restaurant from the childhood in New Jersey that I never had. It has all the trademarks:

  • Dim lighting
  • Red-and-white checkered tablecloths
  • Candles on the tables
  • Burn marks on the plastic menus from the candles on the tables
  • A jolly Italian proprietor
  • Paintings of Italy on the walls
  • Sparkle stars on the ceiling
  • Wicker-wrapped Chianti bottles everywhere

The food is fairly simple and hearty — lots of carbohydrates and garlic. I tend to have the pizza (thin crust, plain pepperoni) and the spaghetti and ravioli combo.

The place seems to close for months at a time, apparently when the owners go back to the old country. This is not a problem if you think of it as a feature rather than a bug: a restaurant that goes to Italy periodically and comes back with whatever absorbed knowledge the trip provides. The Chianti bottles suggest it comes back with wine.

I didn’t grow up in New Jersey. I grew up in the Imperial Valley. But I know what the platonic ideal of a neighborhood Italian restaurant is supposed to feel like, and Marco is it.
