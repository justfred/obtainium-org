Oregon Dunes: Quad Bike

Oregon Dunes National Recreation Area, Spinreel, Oregon. Summer 2004.

The Oregon Dunes are a stretch of coastal sand dunes extending for about forty miles along the Oregon coast south of Florence. They’re up to three miles wide in places. They’re tall enough to block the ocean from view when you’re standing in the middle of them. They’ve been accumulating and moving for about twelve thousand years.

I rented a four-wheeler at Spinreel for an hour.

Quad bikes on sand dunes are approximately the same physical experience as skiing, minus the altitude, the cold, and the chairlift. You go down the steep face, you accelerate on the flat, you climb the other side. The sand flies. The machine makes sense in the environment.

I had a really good time.

The Eel Creek campground nearby is where I slept that night. I burned the LA Times in the fire ring, which is the correct use of a newspaper when you’re in the Oregon Dunes.

The drive up the Oregon coast from California to Washington is, mile for mile, one of the best drives on the continent. The dunes are part of why. You go from old-growth forests to beach to dunes to fishing towns to more forest. Every twenty miles it’s different.

The Vanagon was the right vehicle for this trip. The solar panel on the roof was borderline useful. The fridge was cranky. The bunk beds were adequate. The trip was excellent.
