The Temple Guardian

From photo batch review, May 2026. Burning Man, year uncertain.

The photo is from the playa near the Temple: a person in an elaborate teal deer-creature mask, full costume, standing near the Temple entrance.

We stopped. I took a picture.

Then we cried together for a while. He had had a very intense experience at the Temple, and he shared that. No names were exchanged. No explanation was required. We were two people at the Temple, one of them in a deer mask and one of them with a camera, and we cried together and then went on.

This is a completely normal Burning Man experience. The Temple is where people bring their grief, and the grief accumulates there, and by the end of the week the weight of what everyone has brought is physical in the air. You can stand near it and feel it. You can also meet a person in a deer mask and cry with them for reasons that have nothing to do with anything either of you are going to explain.

The mask helps. Being unrecognizable permits a kind of openness that full faces don’t always allow. He had had an experience. I had had a week. We stood there.

Then we kept walking.
