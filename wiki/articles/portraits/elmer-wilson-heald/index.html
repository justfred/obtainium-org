Elmer Wilson Heald

January 7, 1885 – November 26, 1975. Judge. Veteran. Advocate. Gagalene's third husband; Gramp's stepfather.

Born in Glen Elder, Kansas. Came to Calipatria as a young attorney seeking a beginning to practice his profession. The previous local lawyer, Louis Pat Phillips, had just died. Elmer Heald bought his law practice, his books, and his office equipment. Then he spent some months in Washington DC and came back to court the widow. They married in 1922 in San Diego County and were together 53 years.

The symmetry is hard to miss: he acquired both the law practice and the widow of the man who had owned it.

He went to France as a lieutenant in WWI. He went to Washington to lobby for the Hoover Dam and Imperial Valley water rights, which was central to the Valley’s agricultural survival. He advocated for WWI veterans. He advocated for Japanese Americans during WWII internment — notable for an Imperial County official of that era. He became District Attorney of Imperial County, then Superior Court Judge. His photograph survives in the family records: “Elmer W. Heald — Retired Superior Court Judge.”

He and Gagalene were both strong, demanding personalities. Gramp grew up in that household. Elmer Sr. at least came to his debates.

He died November 26, 1975, fifteen years before Gagalene.
