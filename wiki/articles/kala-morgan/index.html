Kala Morgan

Fred's first serious girlfriend. Pomona College era, ~1986–1988. Not to be confused with Kallah/Kala Devi — that’s Felicia, different person, different decade.

She was at Claremont McKenna, right next door. Opinionated, confident, very intelligent. Got my humor — most of it, anyway. My father hated her. That’s probably not unrelated to why the relationship was what it was.

She was a real presence, not a phase anyone could ignore.

She designed tank-killer smart munitions at Aerojet Electrosystems and called it “Merchants of Death.”

Her get-well note from late 1990: “Have I ever told you you’re the best friend I’ve ever had?” Her 1987 Valentine’s card: “Mere nerds can’t describe how wonderful you are.”

Post-graduation, the relationship didn’t survive. We went different directions.

After her boyfriend Glenn died, I sent letters that were apparently too humorous for the register of grief. That’s likely what finally ended the contact. It’s one of the things I did wrong that I’ve thought about since.

There’s a photo of her sitting on the spare tire on the hood of the 109 Land Rover in the desert, her big husky with blue eyes beside her. No record of what was said.
