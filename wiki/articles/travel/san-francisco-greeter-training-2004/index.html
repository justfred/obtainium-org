San Francisco: Burning Man Greeter Training

August 2004, road trip north. Fred›? spends a day in San Francisco›? — visits Burning Man HQ for Greeter training.

040808.2159 Personal — San Francisco

Left Rebecca’s early in the morning and headed over to Fisherman’s Wharf, for the Alcatraz tour, but it was sold out for the next week. So I cruised down the waterfront and stopped for breakfast at Red’s Java House — a sausage sandwich that wasn’t bad.

040809.1823 — Gualala, CA

Didn’t make it to the Alcatraz tour, which was sold out for the next week, so instead I grabbed breakfast at Red’s Java House out by the Embarcadero — damn fine coffee. And then wandered around San Francisco for a couple of hours till the afternoon Burning Man Greeter meeting. I really love the city, and I could and did just enjoy wasting time there.

I’ve been a greeter before, about two years ago — I did about a ten hour shift one day, got wicked sunburn as well as burnout that lasted about a day, but had an enormously fun time doing it. Your job as greeter is to welcome people to the city, and remind them of the basic health and safety issues: video cameras must be registered; don’t throw anything into the portapotties (we practiced saying “No condoms, no tampons, no baby wipes…” to which I tend to add “…no shrunken human heads, no live chickens…”); and Leave No Trace (“don’t let it hit the ground”). You’re encouraged to have a lot of fun with people and get them into the spirit of the event, and help newbies get their first orientation. Training was fun, though of course lots of extraneous chatter and at least one instance of spontaneous nudity (no, not by me!).

Had a good clam-and-mussel linguine dinner with Uncle Alan, Aunt Carol, and cousin Stephanie; talked about my trip so far, politics and Burning Man (Steph is also going). And breakfast of “Uncle Alan’s Waffles” (another deviation from low-carb, though I did weigh myself this morning and I’m doing fine).
