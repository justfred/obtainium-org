The Perfect Campsite

August 2004. Lolo Pass›?, Idaho-Montana border. On the road to Burning Man.

Nearly the most perfect campsite I can imagine: Lolo Pass, roughly 46.30 North, 114.46 West. I stopped there on the road east through Idaho and Montana.

Level ground. Very quiet. Rain, soft enough not to be inconvenient. I had hot soup and cherry tomatoes and blueberries in the Vanagon with the door open listening to the rain on the trees. A river was visible below the road.

I’d been on the road for weeks by that point. The campsite hit me differently because of that — you appreciate stillness and level ground and quiet when you’ve been moving. The river didn’t care where I’d been or where I was going. The rain didn’t either.

I wrote about it at the time as “nearly the most perfect campsite I can imagine.” I’ve been trying to articulate the “nearly” for twenty years. I think the qualifier was about the weather — a perfect campsite in my imaginary taxonomy has clear skies, and this one had rain. But the rain was correct for that particular place, and the “nearly” might just be the way I leave myself room to be surprised by something better.

I should have stopped at the hot springs nearby. I didn’t. I keep not stopping at hot springs that I later regret missing.
