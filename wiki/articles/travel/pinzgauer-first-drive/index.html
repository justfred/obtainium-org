The Pinzgauer Arrives

January 1, 1999. Vista, California.

I drove a 1972 Pinzgauer for the first time on January 1st, 1999. A Swiss military vehicle — all-wheel-drive, air-cooled, built like a land crab. I’d been looking for one for a while. I picked it up at a place in the Vista area after Kip connected me with Wally Schweier, a German mechanic who knew the type.

The Pinzgauer has more switches and levers than you expect. The carburetors needed adjustment. The fuel gauge needed attention. It was 27 years old when I got it.

I drove home extremely pleased with myself.

Cars are, in general, a lot easier to deal with than people. The Pinz was particularly good at this. It asked very specific, mechanical things of you. You adjusted the carburetors. You kept up with the maintenance. You shuffled your feet a little to find the right gear. In return it went where you pointed it, on terrain where other vehicles stopped.

I took it to Burning Man that year. I drove it to Montana for a work install. I brought it up Tioga Pass in the Sierra Nevada and through the Anza-Borrego desert. It wasn’t a very fast truck and it wasn’t comfortable in the conventional sense. But it was exactly what it said it was, which is more than you can say for most things.

I contemplated vanity plates: PFRED. A combination of Pinz, Fred, and the German word for horse. I never got them. The truck didn’t need the commentary.
