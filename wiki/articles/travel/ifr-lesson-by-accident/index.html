IFR Lesson by Accident

March 2005. Los Angeles›? basin.

I had my first real IFR lesson that day — accidentally, though with my instructor.

We’d planned to fly out to Cable (CCB) and then down to Long Beach (LGB). The weather didn’t hold up, so instead we went to Torrance (TOA). Weather was marginal — if I’d been alone I wouldn’t have gone — but we decided to pop over to Long Beach since it was right next door. I got on the runway fine in VFR. By the time we’d taxied back it was drizzling and closing in.

Jesse, my instructor, filed a Tower Enroute IFR, and I got to fly that back to Santa Monica›?. The clearance was: fly runway heading at 250 to 800 feet, turn to 270, vectors to Santa Monica, climb and maintain 3000 expect 4000 ten minutes out. We headed out over LAX, then east to the VOR approach to SMO. We went in and out of clouds most of the way, dropping into VFR about four miles east of the airport.

I didn’t do any of the radio work for the IFR portion — just flew the plane, which was challenging enough. I’ve flown with a hood before, but never in actual clouds. Actual IFR might be somewhat easier than the blinder hood, as it happens. I get claustrophobic with the hood.

I followed roads on the way back. It’s a hard habit to break when you’re flying visually, even when you’re supposed to be flying instruments. The roads were there and the roads were familiar and the roads were not going to help me in a cloud, but there they were.
