The Mac vs. PC Argument That Would Not Die

June 2004. John Scalzi got hit by adware on his PC. The comments section devolved, as expected.

I couldn’t help but contribute. Might as well put it here, though I’m sure you’re all tired of my broken record by now.

Apologies for continuing the Mac/PC thread, but count me on the Mac side.

I’m a heavy user — I download all sorts of software off the web, mainly from VersionTracker, which checks for viruses itself. I have never had a virus on my Mac. In twenty years. Never. Never had spyware or adware. And I have never needed virus protection software. Beyond this, I still have data I created twenty years ago because I’ve never had my Mac totally crash and die so I couldn’t retrieve the data. I consider this worth tens of thousands of dollars to me. I consider all data on Windows to be disposable unless I have a recent backup, because who knows what’s coming tomorrow.

Purchase price of a Mac is nearly identical to an equivalent Windows PC. I built a Shuttle PC recently for work — around $1,000. I can get an iBook for around $1,000 with equivalent performance, in a laptop with an OS that doesn’t suck.

I don’t even use Windows XP at work — I’m still stuck on Windows 2000, because XP isn’t a technical improvement and the OS itself spies on you. Outlook is the single most offensive program I deal with daily and is a massive unprotected target for viruses. A lot of the spam we receive comes from hijacked Outlook machines.

Apple does a better job considering user needs than Microsoft does. I honestly believe Microsoft doesn’t give a damn about anything but making money; Apple is trying to make quality products and hoping the money will follow. No doubt a worse business strategy.

The biggest difference: I get to actually use my Mac for productive work, and I don’t have to spend time dicking with it. I know there are computer people whose reason for being is messing with cards and IRQs and DLLs and shareware. I just want to use my computer to get work done.

I’ve given up on trying to convert people. And at the same time I refuse to help people with Windows problems, because I know how bad they can get and I tend to take it personally that there’s no way to really fix them.

“Don’t know, it’s Windows. There’s always something wrong. Good luck.”

More of this available on my website. Still true in 2004. Probably still true in ten years.
