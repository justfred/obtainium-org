How Much Is a Billion?

December 2002. Prompted by a news item about the missile defense system.

Does anyone really understand how much a billion dollars is?

I personally believe that most people can’t conceptualize a million dollars, let alone a billion. (In American and modern British usage: one million = 1,000,000; one billion = 1,000,000,000.) Supposedly, most people conceptualize a billion as either two million, or ten million. Most calculators won’t go higher than 99 million.

Let’s give it a try. You can get a fine car for around $25,000. For $1 million, you could buy nice cars for 40 of your family and closest friends. For $1 billion: forty thousand cars. Land Rover sells around 25,000 vehicles in the US per year — so you could buy about one whole year’s worth of all the Rovers sold in the US.

Let’s say a brand-new house costs $250,000. Four houses cost $1 million. Four thousand houses are $1 billion. For the cost of a $100 billion defense shield we could build 400,000 houses — about enough to hold a million people.

Assuming you make $50,000 a year — a great salary — in 20 years you’d have made a million dollars. It would take THIRTY THOUSAND YEARS for you to make a billion dollars. Thirty thousand years ago is roughly when humans began to roam the earth.

There are some 300 million people in the US. Roughly one in three are paying taxes. The missile defense system will cost each of these taxpayers $1,000.

Isn’t there some way we could fight this economically instead of militarily? Give a man a fish, and he’ll eat for a day. Build a multi-billion dollar weapons system and point it at him, and… he’ll probably resent you even more for not just sharing your fish with him in the first place.
