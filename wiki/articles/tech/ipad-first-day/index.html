The iPad Arrives

April 2010, Carlsbad. The device I’d been waiting for since the Newton.

The iPad with a Bluetooth keyboard arrived and it was immediately transformative. The journal entry described it as “the experience I’ve been waiting for since the Newton.”

That’s a specific reference. The Newton MessagePad was Apple’s 1993 attempt at a tablet computer — large, stylus-driven, with handwriting recognition that worked better than its reputation suggested. I’d used one. I’d understood what it was trying to be. And then it got cancelled in 1998, and the concept sat dormant for twelve years while the technology caught up with the vision.

The iPad was the thing the Newton had been pointing toward: a full-sized touchscreen device that you could actually use for sustained work, especially with the keyboard attached. The form factor was right. The size was right. The keyboard worked well enough for extended journaling, which was my primary use case and the one that mattered most.

I considered upgrading to an iPad 2 when it came out in March 2011. The original was still doing what I needed it to do.

The Newton analogy still holds. The iPad did what the Newton wanted to do, with the processing power and the software ecosystem that made it actually possible. Sometimes the technology has to spend a decade becoming capable of supporting the idea.
