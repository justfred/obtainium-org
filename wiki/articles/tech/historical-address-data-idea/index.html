Historical Address Information

February 28, 2007.

Thanks to going to do research yesterday with Mark, I’ve got a whole new idea brewing. It’s nothing someone hasn’t thought of before, but based on a quick web search it’s something that’s not well implemented, if at all.

The initial seed: historical address information. What was at what address, listed back through time. Mark is concerned with pollution sources; other people might be interested in many other views.

Sources of this information? Currently his info comes from old paper reverse address directories — Polk’s and Frye & Smith for example — and old telephone directories. He also uses a number of commercial sources; and maps, and business permits.

What if all these things could be gathered and automatically combined? The obvious candidate for this would be Google. Once said “we want to organize all the world’s information” — this would be a good start. I don’t know what I could do to find out who’s already working on this. One thing I’ve wondered is why Google Earth doesn’t provide better information on the dates of its photos.

One small way to start would be to go through the information and catalog all the dry cleaners in the historical books available for California. Top-of-my-head estimate: about one man-year of cataloging, with a max of about six researchers/typists working for about two months. Would be time-efficient to do gas stations next, or tandem.

Building permits/business licenses would be a lot more effective, but would require working with local governments to collect information.

I haven’t been updating my blog, for several reasons. The login seems to be failing to keep session state — so it forgets that you’re logged in. I think this is a program-specific problem because sessions seem to work fine on the server. I’ll probably reimplement editing with ROR, then see if I can do the same with the other interactive parts of the site, just for giggles.

Then there’s the fact that I’m giving this website out to potential employers, so I start to worry about what I’m posting. Peripherally, I’ve gotten kind of annoyed with the “obtainium” name, mainly because people always spell it wrong — “obtanium.”

Free local wifi service should be standard. City of Encinitas should be providing it for free.
