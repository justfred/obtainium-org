Fred’s Voter Guide — November 3, 2026

California general election. The statewide picks apply to every California voter. The San Diego County section applies countywide. The last section only applies if your ballot matches mine (North County, Vista area). Check your own sample ballot for district races.

Where I’m Coming From

  • I don’t identify with a political party, but I tend to vote with Democrats. If anything, I have moved to the left and am more progressive than before.
  • Taxes should be “equitably painful”: the burden should feel equivalent across income levels, not be equal in dollar amount — or percentage.
  • My default for ballot propositions is no. They bypass the broken political process rather than fixing it, and they make bad law. I’m willing to vote yes if they have sufficient merit.
  • I don’t like endless public bonds increasing public debt, even if I agree with the ends. Initiatives with bonds are doubly no: “Let’s just borrow some more money...”
  • Spending initiatives always sound good, but they should be coming from the general fund or legislative approved expenses.
  • There is little or no voter fraud, but there is significant systematic election fraud and disenfranchisement.
  • Citizens United was terrible law. Speech by individuals should be valued more than speech by corporations.
  • Judges are usually the most difficult, since I’m generally anti “law and order” performances.
  • There are exceptions to every rule — including this one.

State Propositions

Prop Pick Why
1 — Housing bond No More state debt ($500–600M/yr for 25 years). Closest call; it’s a good cause
2 — Rainy day fund Yes Bigger budget reserve; costs nothing, cushions cuts in a recession
3 — High-earner tax made permanent Yes Keeps an existing tax on income over ~$371K; $5–15B/yr for schools and health care
4 — Public campaign financing Yes Lets governments fund campaigns publicly, so candidates depend less on big donors
5 — Recall reform Yes Ends recalls where someone with 20% of the vote replaces someone 49% voted to keep
37 — New-home loans No Initiative and debt; Realtor-funded subsidy that pushes home prices up
38 — Immunology research bond No Initiative and debt ($8.4B); one donor, half to one institute. Fund research through the budget
39 — Voter ID Hard No Rejects far more legitimate ballots than it could ever catch fraud
40 — Billionaire tax Yes One-time 5% on ~200 billionaires; tens of billions for health care
41 — Anti-40 (spending limit) No Written to kill Prop 40 — if both pass, the one with more votes wins
42 — Anti-40 (wealth tax ban) No Also kills 40, and bans wealth taxes permanently
43 — Two-thirds for local initiatives No Lets a one-third minority block taxes that communities vote for
44 — Clinic 90% spending rule No Union-vs-clinic fight dragged onto the ballot; doctors and clinics oppose
45 — CEQA fast-track No Guts environmental review; developer-funded; environmental groups oppose

The 40/41/42 trap: voting No on 41 and 42 matters as much as Yes on 40.
